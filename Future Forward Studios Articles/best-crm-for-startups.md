# Best CRM for Startups: Free and Low-Cost Picks

The best crm for startups depends on how many people need access, how you sell, and what you can afford after the free plan stops fitting. Shortlist HubSpot or Zoho CRM for a small free team, Bigin for a solo starting point with a low-cost paid option, Pipedrive for sales-focused evaluation, and Less Annoying CRM for straightforward monthly pricing.

## What startups need from a CRM

A startup's first CRM should help the team answer three questions: who are we speaking to, what have we agreed, and what happens next? Start with those outcomes before comparing automation, AI assistants, or elaborate dashboards.

If you need a definition first, our [plain-English CRM guide](https://futureforwardstudios.com/what-does-crm-mean/) explains the core terms. The buying decision becomes easier once you can describe your customer process without using software jargon.

### A place for customer conversations

For a practical starting structure, distinguish people, companies, and possible sales. A company may have several contacts, while one contact may discuss more than one project with your business.

Consider a fictional software startup selling to a local retailer. The retailer is the company, the operations manager is a contact, and the proposed subscription is a sales opportunity.

Decide where to record important commitments and who maintains each record. A tool will be difficult to evaluate if the founders disagree about what belongs in it.

### A process that can change

Start with stages reflecting your current sales process, such as enquiry received, discovery completed, proposal sent, and decision pending. Give each stage a clear entry condition.

Avoid building a complicated process around assumptions you have not checked with customers. Keep a short record of why you added a stage so you can revise it when the sales approach changes.

For a founder doing sales personally, a useful initial requirement is simple: every active opportunity has an owner and a next action. Use that standard when evaluating each candidate.

### A realistic budget and an exit route

Look beyond the free starting allowance. Ask what happens when another founder needs access, you hire a salesperson, or you require a specific email workflow.

Also ask how you would retrieve your information if you changed providers. Confirm the export process for contacts, opportunities, activities, notes, and files before depending on the system.

Separate the customer pipeline from investor or recruitment conversations unless you have a clear reason to combine them. Different processes need clear labels and appropriate access, even when you manage them within the same product.

### HubSpot: for a founder and one colleague starting free

HubSpot publishes free CRM functionality for two users and 1,000 contacts. Its [CRM page](https://www.hubspot.com/products/crm) also lists contact, deal, and task management, making it a relevant candidate for evaluating a basic shared sales process.

**Why shortlist it:** the free allowance gives a very small team a defined starting point. It is worth examining when your immediate goal is organising customer conversations and next actions.

**What to watch:** your next essential requirement may involve a paid package. Establish what happens when you need a third user, additional contacts, or a capability outside the free functionality.

Use a fictional enquiry to assess the workflow. Create the contact, record the proposed sale, assign a follow-up, and ask the other person to find the latest commitment.

Do not choose it solely because the starting bill is zero. Obtain a quote for the configuration you would need after your next planned hire.

HubSpot also has a [startup programme](https://www.hubspot.com/startups). Check eligibility and obtain the actual offer before including any programme saving in your budget.

**Suggested fit:** a solo founder or two-person customer-facing team whose current needs match the free allowance.

### Zoho CRM: for three people starting with a shared process

Zoho CRM's [Free Edition](https://www.zoho.com/crm/zohocrm-pricing-free.html) supports up to three users. Its [edition details](https://www.zoho.com/crm/zohocrm-pricing.html) list contact management, tasks, follow-up reminders, and standard reports among the free capabilities.

**Why shortlist it:** the starting user allowance may cover three founders or a founder, salesperson, and administrator. That makes it a useful comparison when a two-person free allowance is insufficient.

**What to watch:** Zoho separates functionality across editions. Make sure the account you evaluate matches the edition you intend to use after any trial access ends.

Give each evaluator a different responsibility in one customer scenario. One person creates the enquiry, another records the discussion, and the third checks whether a follow-up is scheduled.

Record any capability that appears necessary but is missing from your chosen edition. Ask for the relevant paid plan rather than assuming it is a minor configuration change.

The buying question is whether the selected edition supports your process clearly. A large catalogue of possibilities should not distract from that requirement.

**Suggested fit:** a three-person starting team prepared to review edition boundaries carefully.

### Bigin: for a solo founder with a low-cost upgrade option

Bigin offers a Free plan for one user and 500 records. Its [pricing page](https://www.bigin.com/pricing.html) lists Express at US$7 per user monthly on yearly billing, or US$9 on monthly billing.

**Why shortlist it:** you can compare a solo free setup with a clearly priced paid option. Three Express users would cost US$252 for a year at the annual rate, before applicable taxes and extras.

**What to watch:** the free record allowance covers more than contact names. Bigin counts records across contacts, companies, pipelines, activities, products, and notes, as explained in its pricing FAQ.

That distinction matters when planning a migration. Estimate how your intended information would be represented rather than assuming a spreadsheet containing fewer than 500 people will necessarily fit.

During evaluation, create a company, contact, opportunity, and follow-up, then inspect how the account reflects those records. This is a suggested evaluation, not a reported test result.

Compare monthly and annual payment schedules before choosing. A lower annual equivalent is helpful only if the commitment suits your startup's cash position.

**Suggested fit:** one founder starting small, or a budget-conscious team evaluating Express against its essential requirements.

### Pipedrive: for a startup building a repeatable sales process

Pipedrive lists lead, calendar, and pipeline management in Lite. Its [plan comparison](https://www.pipedrive.com/en/pricing) places full email sync and automations in Growth, so the relevant starting package depends on your email workflow.

**Why shortlist it:** it is a useful paid candidate when the immediate buying problem is managing opportunities through a sales process. Evaluate whether your future users can consistently identify the next action on each open deal.

**What to watch:** the appropriate tier matters more than the lowest advertised rate. At the published annual prices, three Lite seats total US$504 yearly, while three Growth seats total US$1,404, before taxes and extras.

Use your real sales stages with fictional customer details. Ask a founder and a future salesperson to move the same opportunity from enquiry to a clear outcome.

If email handling drives the purchase, evaluate that function in the relevant plan. Do not use a demonstration of a broader package as evidence for what Lite includes.

Also list required add-ons separately. A base subscription and a complete working setup are different budgeting questions.

**Suggested fit:** a startup ready to pay for a sales-focused setup after confirming its required tier.

### Less Annoying CRM: for predictable monthly subscription pricing

Less Annoying CRM publishes a single US$15-per-user monthly rate, plus applicable tax. Its [pricing page](https://lessannoyingcrm.com/pricing) lists contacts, pipelines, tasks, email logging, and support among the included services.

**Why shortlist it:** the subscription structure avoids choosing between feature tiers. Three users would cost US$45 monthly before applicable tax.

**What to watch:** the 30-day trial is temporary. Budget for a paid subscription and evaluate your essential integrations before committing.

The straightforward pricing should simplify the financial comparison, but it does not prove that every requirement is supported. Ask specific questions about your website, email, and other software.

Use the trial to assess whether the team can maintain records with minimal instruction. Keep a list of unclear tasks and ask the provider how they should be handled.

If a requirement is absent, establish whether it can be addressed reliably or should rule the product out. Do not build your launch around a hoped-for future feature.

**Suggested fit:** founders who prefer a clear monthly bill and whose needs match the included workflow.

## Free plan limits

### User allowances can decide the shortlist immediately

Count everyone who genuinely needs access, including a founder who reviews opportunities and an administrator who handles enquiries. Use individual accounts rather than planning a shared login to fit a free allowance.

For this shortlist, HubSpot's free allowance covers two users, Zoho CRM's covers three, and Bigin's covers one. Those are starting filters, not complete evaluations of the products.

Ask what happens when you add another person. Confirm whether that changes only the seat cost or requires a different package for the team.

### Contacts and records are different measurements

HubSpot describes its free allowance in contacts, while Bigin's free limit concerns records across several modules. Do not treat those numbers as directly interchangeable.

Sketch the information you intend to store before importing it. Include companies, opportunities, activities, notes, and any other record types relevant to the selected system.

Start with a small sample and inspect the result. Resolve duplicate records and unclear field mapping before moving the rest.

### A trial may show more than the free plan

Record the package name during evaluation and confirm which functions remain afterward. A feature you see during temporary access may belong to a paid tier.

Create a short list of must-haves and check each against the permanent plan you intend to use. Ask the vendor for clarification when the feature comparison is unclear.

Also establish what happens at the end of the trial. Review the downgrade, payment, and data-access terms for that particular product instead of assuming every provider works the same way.

### Integration and administration requirements still matter

A free account is useful only if your essential workflow works. Follow a sample website enquiry through to the responsible person's next task and record any extra service required.

Review permissions, export options, and the work involved in maintaining the setup. Identify one person responsible for stage definitions, duplicate records, and basic questions.

If you are comparing a wider set of products, our [small-business CRM guide](https://futureforwardstudios.com/best-crm-for-small-businesses/) provides another shortlist. Keep the evaluation tied to the team you have and the work you need to improve.

## When to upgrade

### When a specific limitation blocks useful work

Upgrade when you can describe the problem the paid capability will solve. Examples include another employee needing access or a required email workflow being unavailable on the current plan.

Write down what the team does manually today and how the proposed change would help. Avoid buying automation before the underlying process is clear enough to describe.

### When the full cost fits your next stage

Calculate subscriptions, essential extras, setup help, and your team's implementation time. Compare the first payment with the twelve-month total and obtain a second estimate for your expected hiring plan.

Treat temporary discounts separately from the long-term budget. Ask when an offer expires, which subscriptions qualify, and what the next renewal would cost.

If you searched for the best CRM tools for startups 2026, focus on the package you can maintain after the offer ends. A temporary saving should support the decision rather than replace it.

### When the team has adopted the basic process

Review whether active opportunities have owners, meaningful stages, and next actions. Resolve missing information before paying for more reporting around the same incomplete records.

Keep a simple record of what you changed and why. That makes future upgrades easier to assess as the sales approach develops.

The best crm for startups is a system that supports the next stage of your customer process without creating an unmanageable commitment. Start with the smallest suitable plan, confirm its limits, and upgrade when a clear business requirement justifies the cost.

## FAQ

### What is the best free CRM for a startup?

HubSpot, Zoho CRM, and Bigin are worth comparing, depending on user and record requirements. Their published free allowances cover two users and 1,000 contacts, three users, and one user with 500 records respectively; use the linked plan details above to check the full scope.

### Can a startup use a free CRM permanently?

A permanent free plan can remain suitable while your team and required workflow fit its limits. Reassess when you hire, approach an allowance, or need a capability from a paid package.

### How much does a low-cost startup CRM cost?

Among these picks, Bigin Express is listed at US$7 per user monthly billed yearly, or US$9 billed monthly, while Less Annoying CRM lists US$15 per user monthly. Multiply by your required users and add applicable taxes, essential extras, and any implementation costs before comparing totals.

### When should founders move from a spreadsheet to a CRM?

Consider the move when you cannot reliably identify who owns a conversation, what was promised, or when to follow up. Evaluate one complete customer workflow before migrating the whole spreadsheet.
