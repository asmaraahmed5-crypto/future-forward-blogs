# How to Find the Right Keywords for SEO (Step by Step)

Learning how to find the right keywords for SEO starts with your audience’s problems, then uses research tools and the search results to check which pages would help. Choose phrases that fit your business, match the reader’s task, and give you an opportunity to provide a useful answer rather than chasing search volume alone.

## Start with topics

### Step 1: define the reader

Write down who the page should help and what they need to accomplish. “A small business owner choosing a first website platform” is a stronger starting point than “anyone interested in technology.”

Add relevant context, such as their experience level, location where applicable, and the decision they face. Keep assumptions separate from information you have actually learned from customers.

If several audiences need different answers, make that distinction now. A beginner’s setup guide and a developer’s integration reference should not be forced into one brief.

### Step 2: list real problems

Review enquiries, support questions, sales conversations, and other suitable sources you already have. If the site is new, write plausible audience questions and treat them as ideas to investigate.

For a fictional repair business, those might include how to describe a fault, what information a quote needs, and which services are available. These examples do not establish search volume or ranking opportunities.

Group the questions into a few topics. Start with subjects connected to what your business can genuinely explain or provide.

### Step 3: connect topics to the website’s purpose

Ask why each topic belongs on the site. It might support a service decision, explain a useful process, or help readers understand a product category.

Exclude attractive-looking subjects that would take the site away from its audience. Finding keywords for SEO is more useful when the research has a clear boundary.

For the basic concepts, read [what SEO keywords are](https://futureforwardstudios.com/what-are-seo-keywords/). That guide explains how search wording connects with a task.

## Generate ideas

### Step 4: create a small seed list

Write several phrases describing each selected topic. Use the language a beginner or customer would recognize rather than only internal business terminology.

For website-building research, illustrative seeds could include website platforms, free business website, and hosting costs. They begin an investigation rather than settle the final target.

Keep the first list manageable. You should understand the questions behind the phrases before collecting hundreds of variations.

### Step 5: use the appropriate research tool

Google Keyword Planner provides keyword ideas related to words or websites and offers search and advertising-cost estimates. Its current access instructions include completing account setup with billing information for basic idea-generation features. [Keyword Planner documentation](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7337243?hl=en).

Use suitable discovery tools to expand the list, then save only ideas connected to the audience. Record the source, research date, and important market settings.

For tool choices, see [our free keyword research guide](https://futureforwardstudios.com/free-keyword-research-tools/) and [free and paid tools compared](https://futureforwardstudios.com/best-keyword-research-tools/).

### Step 6: inspect existing coverage

Review relevant competitor pages and your own archive. Note the task each page addresses and the questions that still need a useful explanation.

Do not copy another publisher’s headings merely because the page appears in search. Use the research to understand audience needs and define your own contribution.

Follow [our competitor keyword research process](https://futureforwardstudios.com/competitor-keyword-research/) if you need a structured way to compare coverage.

## Check volume and difficulty

### Step 7: treat volume as an estimate

A search estimate is a research input, not a prediction of traffic to your article. Keep its location, period, and provider clear when comparing ideas.

Avoid dismissing a relevant task just because the available estimate is small or missing. Also avoid choosing a broad phrase solely because the displayed number is large.

The best keywords for your website should connect with its purpose. An unrelated audience is not automatically useful simply because more people appear to search for the phrase.

### Step 8: understand difficulty scores

Difficulty metrics are provider-defined estimates of ranking competition. Ahrefs explains both the usefulness and the shortcomings of its Keyword Difficulty metric, rather than presenting it as a foolproof outcome prediction. [Ahrefs difficulty explanation](https://ahrefs.com/blog/keyword-difficulty/).

Use the score as a prompt for further investigation. It cannot fully represent your expertise, the quality of the finished answer, or every aspect of the current results.

Do not assume that a low score means you can publish a thin page and succeed. Likewise, a high score is a reason to investigate the opportunity, not an instruction to abandon every related question.

### Step 9: separate advertising metrics from organic decisions

Google defines Keyword Planner’s competition field in terms of advertisers. That is not the same as an organic ranking difficulty score. [Google’s metric definitions](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/3022575?hl=en).

A high advertising bid also does not establish how much a publisher will earn from an article. If you are researching how to find profitable keywords, connect the topic with a realistic business model rather than treating a bid estimate as projected income.

Keep those distinctions in the research record. They prevent a spreadsheet from giving a false impression of certainty.

## Check the search results

### Step 10: identify the task and format

Search a promising phrase and review the types of results. Notice whether they provide how-to guides, comparisons, service pages, shops, discussions, or working tools.

Ask whether the page you can create serves that need. An article describing a calculator may not satisfy someone who wants to perform a calculation immediately.

Treat your review as a dated observation rather than a permanent rule about the query. Keep the market and other relevant conditions in mind.

### Step 11: find a distinct contribution

Read representative pages and note what a beginner needs to understand or do next. Look for opportunities to explain a confusing step, supply an appropriate example, or organize a decision more clearly.

Do not define the contribution merely as a longer article. More words help only when they add information the task requires.

Google’s starter guidance emphasizes useful content and understandable organization. Apply those principles rather than mechanically repeating related phrases. [Google SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide).

### Step 12: decide whether to update or create

Check whether an existing page already serves the same central task. Improving that page may be more appropriate than publishing another near-duplicate URL.

For example, [our website platform guide](https://futureforwardstudios.com/website-building-platforms/) helps readers choose an approach, while [our business-site walkthrough](https://futureforwardstudios.com/free-business-website/) explains a setup process. The task difference gives the pages distinct roles.

For a site you manage, Search Console reports on queries and pages associated with your own search performance. Use that evidence as another input when evaluating existing coverage. [Search Console performance guide](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7576553?hl=en).

## Build your keyword list: how to find the right keywords for SEO

### Step 13: group by the answer needed

Put close variations together when one clear page can satisfy them. Separate topics when the audience’s purpose or required explanation changes substantially.

The examples below illustrate grouping decisions, not validated keyword data.

| Candidate topic | Reader’s task | Page direction |
|---|---|---|
| Free business website setup | Publish a useful first version | A practical walkthrough |
| Website platform types | Choose an editing and publishing approach | A decision guide |
| Hosting renewal costs | Understand the full payment commitment | A cost comparison |
| Shared hosting meaning | Understand a hosting arrangement | An explainer |

One topic group can include several natural wordings. It does not need one article per exported phrase.

### Step 14: record enough context

For each group, save the audience, task, main phrase, supporting ideas, proposed URL, and page type. Add the research source and date.

Then record the distinct contribution and evidence required. This helps a writer understand what the page must accomplish rather than simply supplying words to repeat.

Leave unknown metrics unknown until you have an appropriate source. A blank cell is better than an invented estimate.

### Step 15: prioritize a realistic publishing plan

Assess relevance, usefulness, available expertise, research effort, and maintenance needs. Choose a small set you can complete properly.

A fictional business might prioritize a clear service explanation before a broad industry comparison. The decision follows the business’s audience and resources rather than the biggest volume figure.

Assign one owner and a next step to each approved brief. Keep rejected ideas with a short reason so the same unsuitable topics do not repeatedly return.

### Work through one complete brief

Imagine your chosen reader is a service business owner who needs a website but has never published one. The broad topic is website creation, while the specific task is building a useful first version without managing code.

The brief could require essential pages, an appropriate contact method, platform restrictions, and costs that may appear later. A helpful contribution would be a launch sequence that includes checking the published customer journey.

Next, identify the evidence needed: current platform documentation and accurate instructions for the chosen setup. Do not fill missing plan information with assumptions simply because the outline looks ready.

Decide which related topics belong elsewhere. A detailed ranking of every builder would expand the task, while a link to a separate comparison can give readers the choice without overwhelming the walkthrough.

Finally, write the completion check: a beginner should understand what to prepare, what to build, and what to test before sharing the website. That is a more useful target than repeating a phrase a certain number of times.

This example shows how research becomes an editorial decision without invented volume or ranking numbers. Use the same sequence for another topic, adapting the audience and task rather than copying the outline mechanically.

### Review and improve after publication

Record meaningful changes and review relevant evidence over time. Avoid treating every new phrase or short-term movement as a reason to rewrite the whole page.

Use [our SEO report template](https://futureforwardstudios.com/seo-report-generator/) to connect observations with actions. Keep performance monitoring separate from assumptions about why something changed.

Once you understand how to find the right keywords for SEO, the goal is a useful, maintainable content plan. Start with audience problems, check the evidence, and choose pages that answer distinct questions well.

## FAQ

### How do I find SEO keywords for a new website?

Begin with audience questions and topics connected to what the site offers, then investigate wording with research tools and the search results. Build a small set of focused page briefs rather than a large list of unrelated phrases.

### Should I target the keywords with the highest search volume?

Not automatically: relevance, intent, and your ability to provide a useful answer matter to the decision. Treat the estimate as one input rather than a promise of traffic.

### What keyword difficulty should a beginner target?

There is no universal cutoff that guarantees a suitable opportunity. Understand the provider’s metric, inspect the actual results, and choose a task your site can answer credibly.

### Does every keyword need a separate article?

No: related phrases can belong on one page when they require the same central answer. Create a separate page when the task or information needed is substantially different.
