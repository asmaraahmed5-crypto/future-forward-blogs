# How to Find Your Competitors' Keywords (Free and Paid Ways)

Competitor keyword research starts by finding websites that answer your audience’s questions, then comparing their topics and search visibility with your own. Use free page inspection and keyword suggestions for initial ideas, or paid gap tools for a broader comparison, before selecting relevant opportunities you can serve usefully.

## Find your real competitors

### Start with searches your customers would make

Write a short list of problems your website helps solve. For a business publishing website guides, those might include choosing a platform, understanding hosting, and comparing renewal costs.

Search those topics and note the websites appearing for the same tasks you intend to address. Separate article publishers from software landing pages and online stores when their purposes differ.

Your search competitors may not be the businesses you compete with commercially. A forum, an independent publication, or a provider’s help center can answer the same question as your planned article.

### Choose relevant pages, not just famous domains

Start with a manageable shortlist and save representative URLs. A large website may have sections unrelated to your audience, so comparing the whole domain can produce a distracting list.

For each page, record the audience, question, format, and intended next action. This gives the research context beyond a collection of competitor keywords.

A fictional local photographer, for example, should distinguish a nearby service page from a national tutorial about photography equipment. Both mention photography, but they serve different tasks.

### Record the market and date

Note the search location, language, and research date. If a tool provides device or country settings, keep them consistent across the comparison.

Treat the result as a snapshot to investigate. It does not establish a permanent position or the exact traffic another website receives.

## Methods and tools for competitor keyword research

### Free method: inspect the page itself

Read the main heading, introduction, section headings, navigation, and linked resources. Write a plain-language summary of what the page helps the reader accomplish.

Note phrases that describe the task naturally. Do not assume that every repeated word is a deliberate keyword target or that appearing on the page proves a ranking.

Use browser search to find a particular phrase within the visible content. Keep the page’s actual explanation separate from what an external tool estimates about its performance.

### Free method: use a site-restricted search

A query such as `site:example.com hosting renewal` can help you discover pages on a domain associated with that topic. Replace the example domain and words with the website and subject you are investigating.

Google explains that `site:` results are not necessarily exhaustive. Their order should not be treated as a ranking report, and an absent result does not prove that the page is missing from Google’s index. [Google’s site-operator documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/search-operators/all-search-site).

Use this method to locate content worth reading. Do not use the result count as a precise measure of a competitor’s archive or traffic.

### Free tool route: start with a website in Keyword Planner

Google Keyword Planner’s “Discover new keywords” workflow includes a “Start with a website” option. Google looks for phrases related to the entered site’s content; these are suggestions, not a confirmed list of its organic rankings. [Keyword Planner instructions](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7337243?hl=en).

The current help page also specifies account setup requirements, including billing information for basic idea-generation access. Review the setup and campaign settings rather than assuming research should authorize advertising activity.

Save relevant suggestions and discard phrases unrelated to your own audience. A competitor’s wider product range may create ideas that do not belong on your website.

### Paid route: use Ahrefs Content Gap

Ahrefs describes Content Gap as a comparison of keywords competitors rank for against those associated with your own site. It supports comparing multiple competitors and filtering by how many of them appear for a phrase. [Ahrefs Content Gap](https://ahrefs.com/content-gap).

Use it to create an investigation list, not an automatic publishing queue. Check the current account and plan requirements before purchasing access for a specific report.

A competitor keyword tool can make comparison easier, but its output still needs relevance and intent checks. An estimated gap is a lead to investigate rather than proof that your website needs another page.

### Paid route: use Semrush Keyword Gap

Semrush documents groups including “Missing,” where all entered competitors have rankings and your site does not, and “Weak,” where your site ranks below all entered competitors. It also describes database, device, date, and other filters. [Semrush Keyword Gap guide](https://www.semrush.com/kb/28-keyword-gap).

Check which comparison and export capabilities your selected account provides. Keep settings consistent and inspect the URLs associated with promising phrases.

For a wider overview of SEO competitor analysis tools and research options, see [our free and paid keyword tool comparison](https://futureforwardstudios.com/best-keyword-research-tools/).

## Keyword gap analysis

### Remove gaps that do not belong to your business

Start by excluding irrelevant products, unsuitable locations, unrelated audiences, and tasks you cannot answer responsibly. Do not pursue a phrase merely because several competitors cover it.

Next, separate brand-navigation searches from broader informational needs. A person trying to reach another company’s login page is not automatically an appropriate reader for your article.

Competitive keyword analysis becomes useful when the remaining list matches your site’s purpose. A shorter relevant list is easier to turn into useful work.

### Check what the searcher needs

Review the current results for each promising phrase. Identify whether they mainly provide tutorials, comparisons, service pages, discussions, or working tools.

Compare that task with what you can provide. An explanatory article may not satisfy someone looking to use a live calculator or access a specific product.

Also consider whether the information needs updating frequently. A pricing comparison creates different maintenance demands from a stable definition.

### Compare against your existing content

Search your own archive before creating a new URL. One existing guide may already address the task and simply need a clearer explanation or a missing example.

For a website you manage, Search Console reports on your own search performance, including queries, pages, clicks, and impressions. Use it as another input when checking existing coverage. [Search Console performance documentation](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7576553?hl=en).

Keep that evidence separate from third-party estimates. Your own report does not reveal a competitor’s private search data.

### Build a gap-to-action table

The following examples illustrate decisions, not findings from an actual competitor audit.

| Reader’s task | Existing coverage to inspect | Possible action |
|---|---|---|
| Understand shared hosting | Your hosting explainer | Improve the explanation before creating another definition page |
| Compare introductory and renewal costs | Your cost comparison | Add a worked budget and current supporting sources |
| Choose a website platform | Your platform guide | Clarify which route fits each audience |
| Complete an unrelated specialist task | No relevant expertise or scope | Exclude the idea from the current plan |

The action should follow the need. A keyword gap does not always require a new article.

## Turn gaps into a content plan

### Prioritize a useful contribution

For each candidate, write one sentence explaining what your page would add. That could be a clear setup sequence, a practical worksheet, or a better-supported answer to a specific question.

Then assess audience fit, research effort, and maintenance. Avoid treating a low difficulty score as a substitute for this judgment.

For example, a cost guide could explain the full upfront payment rather than repeating a monthly headline. A platform guide could compare editing responsibilities instead of adding another unsupported ranking.

### Choose between updating and creating

Update an existing page when it already serves the same central task. Create a separate page when the audience needs a substantially different answer.

Group close wording variations into the appropriate brief. Do not turn every exported phrase into a near-duplicate URL.

Connect supporting pages where the relationship helps the reader. At Future Forward Studios, a [website platform guide](https://futureforwardstudios.com/website-building-platforms/) can lead to [a practical business-site walkthrough](https://futureforwardstudios.com/free-business-website/) rather than repeating that walkthrough in full.

### Write an actionable brief

Record the audience, task, proposed title, URL, key sections, required evidence, and related pages. Include the distinctive contribution so the writer knows what useful work the article must perform.

Keep research observations separate from conclusions. “Several sampled pages discuss renewal costs” is different from claiming that a particular missing section caused their rankings.

### Work through one opportunity before scaling

Imagine your research suggests that beginners struggle to compare hosting subscriptions. First, identify whether they need a definition, a price comparison, or instructions for choosing a billing term.

If the chosen task is comparing the full cost, inspect your existing pricing guide. It may already provide the right destination, with an opportunity to add a clearer calculation rather than launch another article.

Your brief could specify a fictional budget example, current provider sources, and a distinction between monthly equivalents and money due today. It should also require clear limits on what the numbers establish.

Before scheduling the work, confirm who will maintain changing prices. That responsibility is part of the content decision, not something to leave until the information becomes outdated.

### Keep a compact research record

Save the competitor URL, the audience question, the research source, and the date. Add the relevant market and any tool settings that affect the comparison.

Write down the proposed action and why it makes sense. This lets another editor understand the decision without reopening every page you reviewed.

Also keep a short exclusion note for tempting but unsuitable ideas. Remembering why a phrase was rejected prevents the same irrelevant topic from repeatedly returning to the publishing queue.

### Review after publication

Track meaningful updates and review the relevant evidence over time. Do not assume a short-term change proves that copying a competitor’s topic succeeded or failed.

Use what you learn to improve explanations and navigation. Your aim is to serve the reader more effectively, not reproduce another website’s archive.

Competitor keyword research works best as a discovery and prioritization process. Find relevant competitors, verify the task behind each opportunity, and turn genuine gaps into content your own audience can use.

## FAQ

### How can I find competitors’ keywords for free?

Inspect their pages, use site-restricted searches, and investigate related suggestions with an available keyword tool. These methods reveal topics and potential targets, not a complete verified list of organic rankings or traffic.

### Which paid tools can compare keyword gaps?

Ahrefs Content Gap and Semrush Keyword Gap both document competitor-comparison workflows. Check the current plan access, settings, and exports before subscribing for a specific task.

### Should I target every keyword a competitor ranks for?

No: filter for your audience, business scope, search intent, and ability to provide a useful answer. Some gaps are irrelevant, and others are better addressed by improving an existing page.

### Can I copy competitors’ article outlines?

Use competitor website analysis to understand the questions readers need answered, then develop your own explanation and supporting material. A useful brief should define a distinct contribution rather than reproduce another publisher’s structure.
