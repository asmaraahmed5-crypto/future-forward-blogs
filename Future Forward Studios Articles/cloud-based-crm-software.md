# Cloud-Based CRM Software: Best Options Compared

HubSpot, Zoho CRM, Pipedrive, and Salesforce are worth evaluating for different small-business needs: starting free, organising sales, or bringing several customer-facing activities together. The right cloud based crm software is the one that handles your daily follow-ups at a sustainable total cost, rather than the one with the longest feature list.

## What cloud CRM is

A cloud CRM is a customer relationship management tool delivered online, with the provider hosting the software instead of your business running it on its own server. Your team uses it to organise customer information and coordinate work around those relationships. Salesforce explains this delivery model in its [guide to CRM](https://www.salesforce.com/crm/what-is-crm/).

For a small business, the practical goal is straightforward: know who contacted you, what they need, who owns the conversation, and what happens next. Before comparing crm programs, write down where that process currently breaks.

Perhaps enquiries arrive through your website, but nobody records whether someone replied. Perhaps a quote sits in an employee's inbox while the owner assumes the customer has already decided.

### Understand the records you will manage

Think of a contact as a person, an account as a business, and a deal as a possible sale. An activity is something your team needs to do, such as calling a prospect or following up on a proposal.

Terminology differs between products, so use these as working definitions when evaluating a platform. Ask how each system connects a person, their company, an opportunity, and its next action.

For example, a fictional design studio might have one company record for a local bakery, two contacts at that company, and one deal for a website redesign. The next activity could be a call to confirm the project scope.

That structure is more useful than storing everything in one notes field. It gives the team specific information to maintain and specific questions to answer.

### Start with a process, then choose software

Describe your sales process in plain language before building it inside a CRM. A service business might use enquiry received, requirements confirmed, proposal sent, decision pending, and won or lost.

Give each stage a clear meaning. “Proposal sent” should mean that the customer actually received a proposal, rather than that someone intends to write one.

Also decide what your CRM should not manage. A simple sales process does not automatically require project delivery, bookkeeping, and customer support to move into the same system.

If your enquiries start on a website, first make the contact journey clear. Our guide to [building a free business website](https://futureforwardstudios.com/free-business-website/) explains the pages and contact information to establish before adding more software.

## Reviews

### HubSpot: consider it for a small free starting point

HubSpot's [free CRM page](https://www.hubspot.com/products/crm) lists contact, deal, and task management, alongside a free allowance of two users and 1,000 contacts. Those published limits make it a sensible candidate for an owner and one colleague who want to organise follow-ups before committing to a subscription.

The strongest reason to evaluate it is a small, well-defined starting requirement. Import a modest sample, create a deal, assign a task, and see whether the people doing the work understand the process.

The main caution is growth beyond the free allowance. A free starting point should not replace a review of the paid plan you would need when adding colleagues or changing the workflow.

Use the [Sales Hub pricing page](https://www.hubspot.com/pricing/sales) to examine the relevant paid package rather than assuming every sales feature belongs to the free CRM. Ask for an estimate based on your actual users and required capabilities.

**Choose it for your shortlist if:** you have no more than two initial users and a clear reason to start with the free allowance.

**Look elsewhere first if:** you already know your team exceeds that allowance and have not budgeted for the appropriate paid package.

During evaluation, ask a colleague to find a contact, understand the latest conversation, and identify the next task without help. That exercise is a proposed buying check, not a claim about HubSpot's usability.

### Zoho CRM: consider it for a three-person free team

Zoho publishes a [Free Edition for three users](https://www.zoho.com/crm/zohocrm-pricing.html), with contact management, tasks, reminders, and standard reports among its listed capabilities. That makes it worth considering when three people need to share a basic sales process without an immediate subscription.

The useful comparison is between your required workflow and the edition that supports it. Zoho's pricing page separates free and paid editions, so avoid judging the Free Edition by a demonstration of a higher package.

For a practical evaluation, give each person a different responsibility. One creates the enquiry, another handles the call, and the owner reviews the outcome.

Check whether your proposed record structure stays understandable after those handoffs. If everyone creates their own naming rules, fix the process before adding custom fields or automation.

The main buying risk is choosing an edition before documenting your requirements. A capability appearing somewhere in the Zoho product family does not establish that it belongs to the particular subscription you are considering.

**Choose it for your shortlist if:** three free users would cover the starting team and you want to evaluate a shared sales database.

**Look elsewhere first if:** your deciding requirement is a specific integration or advanced workflow that you have not confirmed in the selected edition.

Ask for a written mapping between your must-have requirements and the proposed plan. That is more useful than a general promise that the platform can grow with you.

### Pipedrive: consider it for a defined sales pipeline

Pipedrive's [Lite plan](https://www.pipedrive.com/en/pricing) lists lead, calendar, and pipeline management at US$14 per seat monthly when billed annually. The same page places full email sync and automations in Growth, priced at US$39 per seat monthly on annual billing.

That distinction matters if your main complaint is copying information between email and your sales records. Evaluate the plan containing the required email workflow rather than choosing the lowest advertised price first.

For three seats, the published annual rates work out to US$504 for Lite or US$1,404 for Growth before applicable taxes and extras. These figures are arithmetic based on the listed annual seat prices, not a customised quote.

A good evaluation scenario is an enquiry that moves through qualification, proposal, and decision. Ask each salesperson to record an activity and explain why the deal belongs in its current stage.

The main caution is total scope. Pipedrive's pricing page also lists optional add-ons, so identify which requirements belong to the selected subscription and which need an additional purchase.

**Choose it for your shortlist if:** you are buying primarily to manage sales opportunities and follow-up activities.

**Look elsewhere first if:** your central requirement is a broader service or marketing workflow that you have not checked against the product and add-ons.

Keep the evaluation focused on whether the team can maintain the pipeline consistently. Attractive dashboards have little value if the underlying deal stages are unreliable.

### Salesforce Starter Suite: consider it for a broader customer workflow

Salesforce describes Starter Suite as bringing sales, service, marketing, and commerce together for small businesses. Its [published starting price](https://www.salesforce.com/small-business/pricing/) is US$25 per user monthly, with monthly or annual billing available.

For three users, that starting rate is US$75 monthly, or US$900 across twelve months, before taxes and any additional charges. Ask which required functions are included before treating that calculation as your full budget.

The reason to shortlist it is a requirement that crosses customer-facing roles. For example, a fictional training company might want the sales enquiry and a later customer request to remain connected in its working process.

Build a demonstration around that handoff. Ask the vendor to show how your selected package handles the exact scenario, rather than touring unrelated features from other editions.

The main caution is package boundaries. Salesforce's [suite comparison](https://www.salesforce.com/en-us/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/documents/small-business/comparison-chart-starter-prosuite-features-us.pdf) distinguishes its small-business offerings, so establish what you are buying before planning the rollout.

**Choose it for your shortlist if:** you want to evaluate several customer-facing activities within one suite and have someone responsible for implementation.

**Look elsewhere first if:** your immediate problem is only a short contact list and your team has no capacity for a wider setup project.

Request a demonstration using your process language. A tool should help the team understand the work rather than require everyone to adopt an unfamiliar process without a reason.

## How to choose

### Write a one-page requirements list

Start with five must-haves and a separate list of optional improvements. For a small service company, essentials might include shared contacts, deal ownership, follow-up dates, an email workflow, and a usable export.

Describe outcomes instead of feature names. “A new website enquiry reaches the responsible person” is clearer than “advanced lead management.”

Write down the current problem beside each requirement. If nobody can explain which problem a feature solves, leave it out of the first buying decision.

### Evaluate the same scenario in each system

Use fictional records during the initial evaluation. Create a contact, attach a company, open an opportunity, assign an owner, and schedule the next action.

Then move the opportunity through your proposed stages and mark it won or lost. Ask whether another person can understand the record without reading a separate explanation.

Use a simple scorecard with pass, partial, or fail against each must-have. Record the exact plan used so that trial access to broader functionality does not distort the comparison.

Do not turn this scorecard into a universal ranking. A system that fits your consultancy may be a poor fit for a retailer with different customer workflows.

### Check integrations in both directions

List the systems that must exchange information with your CRM. These might include your business email, website forms, appointment booking, or accounting software.

Ask what moves between them, how often it moves, and what happens when the connection fails. Confirm whether the connection requires a particular CRM plan, another subscription, or paid setup.

For website enquiries, follow one sample submission from the form to the assigned person and scheduled follow-up. Our guide to [website building platforms](https://futureforwardstudios.com/website-building-platforms/) can help you review the website side of that decision.

### Calculate a realistic first-year budget

Use this planning formula: subscription charges plus required extras, setup, migration, and training. Add the time your own team will spend cleaning records and learning the system.

Compare the amount due now with the annual cost. A price expressed per month can still require a full year's payment upfront, as Pipedrive's annual billing examples demonstrate.

Request a second estimate for your likely team size next year. Include the plan changes triggered by your next essential requirement, rather than assuming today's entry package will remain sufficient.

### Clean your records before migration

Remove obvious duplicates, standardise company names, and decide which person owns each active opportunity. Separate active prospects from old records that no longer need day-to-day attention.

Import a small sample first and inspect the field mapping. Check names, email addresses, notes, owners, and dates before moving the rest.

Also rehearse an export before committing. Ask how you would retrieve contacts, opportunities, activities, notes, and attachments, and inspect what the selected package actually provides.

### Assign an owner and keep the launch small

Choose one person to maintain field definitions, answer basic questions, and coordinate changes. That person needs enough time to handle the responsibility, even if administration is only part of their job.

At launch, require only the information needed to manage the next action. Add fields when they support a clear decision, rather than because the software allows them.

Review records with the team after the first working cycle. Look for missing owners, unclear stages, and overdue tasks before adding more crm management tools.

### Check access and customer communication

Decide who should view, edit, export, and delete records, then confirm the selected plan can support those rules. Review account recovery, employee offboarding, and the provider's security documentation before importing live customer data.

Keep sales follow-up separate from permission to send marketing messages. Recording a contact in a CRM should not become an automatic instruction to subscribe that person to every campaign.

If AI features are part of the purchase, ask what information they use and which actions need approval. Require a person to review customer-facing messages during your initial rollout.

### Make the final decision

Choose the system that passes your essential workflow checks at a cost you can maintain. Start with HubSpot or Zoho when their free allowances match your team, evaluate Pipedrive for a sales-focused process, and consider Salesforce Starter Suite for a broader customer workflow.

The best cloud based crm software for your business should make ownership and follow-ups clear. A modest setup that the team maintains is a better purchase than an ambitious configuration nobody keeps current.

## FAQ

### What is cloud-based CRM software used for?

It helps a business organise customer information and manage work around those relationships through an online system. Typical evaluation tasks include recording enquiries, assigning follow-ups, and tracking potential sales, although included capabilities depend on the product and plan.

### Is there free cloud CRM software for small businesses?

Yes: HubSpot publishes a free allowance of two users and 1,000 contacts, while Zoho CRM offers a Free Edition for three users. Check their [current](https://www.hubspot.com/products/crm) [plan details](https://www.zoho.com/crm/zohocrm-pricing.html) against your requirements before deciding that free access covers the whole workflow.

### How much does a cloud CRM cost for three users?

At the published annual Lite rate, three Pipedrive seats total US$504 per year before applicable taxes and extras. Three Salesforce Starter Suite users at its US$25 monthly starting rate total US$900 over twelve months, so compare included capabilities alongside the subscription arithmetic.

### Can I move from a spreadsheet to a CRM?

Plan the move by cleaning your spreadsheet, defining fields, and checking the chosen product's import instructions. Import a small sample first, inspect the results, and keep a copy of the original data until you have confirmed the migration.
