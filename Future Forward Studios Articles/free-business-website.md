# How to Create a Free Business Website (No Coding)

Learning how to create a website for business for free starts with choosing a suitable publishing platform, adding clear service information, and testing a working contact method. Build a small site first, then consider paid features when a specific business need justifies them.

## Pages every business site needs

### A homepage that explains the offer

Your homepage should answer three questions: What do you do, who do you help, and what should visitors do next? If your work is location-dependent, make the service area clear.

For example, a fictional cleaning business could write: “Home cleaning for apartments and family houses in our local service area.” Replace the broad location with the business’s actual coverage before publishing.

Follow the opening with a short service summary and a contact action. Avoid competing buttons that send visitors in several unrelated directions.

### A service or work page

Describe what a customer receives, who the service suits, and any important exclusions. Explain the next step, whether that is requesting a quote, sending project details, or arranging a conversation.

Use real examples of your work where you have permission to show them. If you display a demonstration project, label it as a sample rather than presenting it as paid client work.

A service description is more useful when it addresses a common question. For a photographer, that might be what the customer needs to prepare before requesting a quote.

### An About page

Explain who runs the business and how you work. Use specific, supportable information instead of broad claims about being the most trusted or experienced provider.

You do not need a long company history. A short introduction with a clear explanation of your approach can give customers useful context.

### A Contact page and relevant privacy information

Provide a contact method you actually monitor. If you show opening hours, a location, or a service area, check that those details are accurate.

If the site collects enquiries or uses third-party services, prepare privacy information that reflects those activities. Keep it specific to the website’s actual operation rather than copying another company’s policy.

## Choose a free platform: how to create a website for business for free

### Start with your essential requirement

Write down the feature that would prevent the site from working if it were missing. For a simple service business, that might be an enquiry form, a gallery, or an easy-to-update service page.

Distinguish that requirement from optional extras. You can launch useful information without an animated homepage or a complicated booking system.

### Consider Wix for a visual first version

Wix provides free website creation and publishing with templates and design tools. Its free sites use a Wix address, and upgrading is required to connect your own domain or remove Wix advertising. [Wix free website guide](https://support.wix.com/en/article/building-a-website-for-free).

Consider this route if you want to assemble a business-site draft visually. Before committing, check whether the specific contact or business feature you need belongs to the free offering.

### Consider WordPress.com for regular publishing

WordPress.com’s free plan includes hosting, unlimited pages and posts, and 1 GB of media storage. Its free version uses a WordPress.com address, while custom domains and plugin installation are paid-plan features. [WordPress.com plan features](https://wordpress.com/support/plan-features/).

This is worth investigating if guides or articles will be a regular part of the business website. Try creating one service page and one article before deciding whether the editing routine suits you.

### Consider Google Sites for straightforward information

Google Sites supports creating sites for personal or business use. It also lets you insert content such as Google Forms and other Google files. [Google Sites creation guide](https://support.google.com/sites/answer/98081?hl=en), [Google Sites content guide](https://support.google.com/sites/answer/90569?hl=en).

Consider it when the website’s job is presenting a small amount of clear information. Check the entire visitor journey, including access to any inserted files, before sharing the address.

For more detail on these choices, read [our free website maker comparison](https://futureforwardstudios.com/best-free-website-makers/). Choose based on your repeated tasks rather than the most attractive sample design.

## Build the pages

### Prepare the content before editing

Write your service summary, About text, and contact information in a separate document. Collect images you own or have permission to use, and give the files descriptive names.

Preparing material first makes template selection easier. You can judge whether a layout fits your business instead of trying to reshape the business around sample content.

### Create the account and select a layout

Use an account the business controls. Keep ownership clear if a freelancer, colleague, or family member helps build the site.

Choose a layout close to your planned page structure. Remove sample testimonials, unsupported customer counts, and any section you cannot fill honestly.

### Build the homepage first

Add your business name, a clear headline, a brief explanation, and the main action. Then add only the supporting information needed to help a visitor decide what to do.

For a fictional repair service, that might mean service categories, coverage information, and an enquiry link. Do not add a booking button unless an actual booking process exists behind it.

### Add the supporting pages

Create the service, About, and Contact pages using the prepared material. Make headings descriptive so someone scanning the page can find the information they need.

Keep paragraphs short and explain unfamiliar terms. Customers should not need industry knowledge to understand your basic offer.

### Review the site as a new customer

Ask someone unfamiliar with the business to read the homepage and explain what you sell. If their answer differs from your offer, revise the headline and service summary before adding more content.

Next, ask them to find one service detail and the contact method. Notice where they hesitate, especially if an important answer is hidden behind an unclear menu label.

Use those observations to make a short improvement list. Fix confusing wording, missing information, and broken actions before making small decorative changes.

This review does not need to become a large project. A few focused tasks can expose problems you overlook because you already know the business.

### Connect the pages and check mobile layouts

Use straightforward navigation labels such as Services, About, and Contact. Link from the homepage to the relevant service information and from each service page to the next action.

Open every page on a phone. Check for cropped images, crowded buttons, and headings that break awkwardly across several lines.

Keep a separate copy of your finished text and images. That gives you material to reuse if you later change platforms or redesign the site.

## Add contact form and basic SEO

### Keep the enquiry form short

Ask only for information needed to respond. A simple initial enquiry might need a name, reply address, and a short description of the request.

Use labels that explain what to enter. Avoid making visitors provide a phone number, budget, or lengthy questionnaire unless you need that information at this stage.

Google Sites supports inserting a Google Form through the Insert panel. Google warns that restricted sharing permissions can prevent visitors from seeing inserted files, so check the published result while logged out. [Google’s embedding instructions](https://support.google.com/sites/answer/90569?hl=en).

For another builder, confirm the form’s current plan availability before relying on it. If the required form is unavailable, decide whether a monitored email link is an acceptable first contact method.

### Test the full contact journey

Submit an enquiry using an address you control. Confirm that it reaches the right destination and that you can reply successfully.

Check any confirmation message so the visitor understands what happened. Do not promise a response time the business cannot consistently meet.

### Make the page topic clear

Write a descriptive title and main heading for each page. For example, “Product Photography for Independent Shops” communicates more than “Our Solutions.”

Use service and location wording where it helps explain the offer. Avoid inserting the same search phrase repeatedly into headings or forcing awkward keywords into otherwise clear sentences.

Google’s SEO guidance emphasizes useful content, descriptive titles, understandable links, and helping search engines discover pages. These are good starting points, but they do not guarantee rankings. [Google SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide).

### Publish and review the live site

After publishing, open the address without being logged into the builder. Follow the navigation, read the service details, and repeat the contact test.

Google Sites lets you decide who can see the published site. Check those settings deliberately rather than assuming publication makes every part accessible to everyone. [Google Sites publishing guide](https://support.google.com/sites/answer/6372880?hl=en).

## Costs that appear later

### Your own domain

A custom domain is a separate decision from creating the pages. Free Wix and WordPress.com addresses are useful for starting, but their custom-domain routes require a paid plan.

Google Sites supports connecting a domain you own, with setup handled by the site owner; work or school accounts may need administrator involvement. Domain registration and renewal are still separate from building the website. [Google Sites custom-domain guide](https://support.google.com/sites/answer/9068867?hl=en).

### Extra functions and ongoing work

Before upgrading, identify the problem you need solved. That might be a required integration, more storage, removal of platform advertising, or a different publishing workflow.

Compare the full billing commitment and renewal terms, not just an introductory headline. Include domain renewal, email, and required add-ons if they are part of your planned setup.

Also account for maintenance time. Someone must update service details, respond to enquiries, and check that the contact method still works.

### Decide when spending makes sense

A free website for business can be a useful first version when its limitations fit the job. Upgrade when you can explain the benefit the spending provides, rather than because a dashboard advertises more features.

If you are deciding how to create a website for business for free, start with accurate information, a manageable platform, and a tested contact journey. Those essentials give you a working foundation you can improve as the business learns what customers need.

## FAQ

### Can I create a business website for free without coding?

Yes: Wix, WordPress.com, and Google Sites provide website-building workflows that do not require writing website code. Their free-plan limits differ, so choose around the pages and contact method your business needs.

### Can I use my business domain on a free website?

Wix and WordPress.com require a paid plan for their custom-domain options. Google Sites supports connecting a domain you already own, but registration, renewal, and any account-specific requirements remain separate.

### Do I need a contact form to launch?

A contact form is useful, but a monitored email link may serve as an initial contact method. Whatever you choose, test it from the published website and confirm that an enquiry can receive a reply.

### Will my free business website appear on Google?

Publishing a publicly accessible site does not guarantee that Google will index it or rank it for a particular search. Follow the basic SEO steps above and make the pages useful to the customers you want to reach.
