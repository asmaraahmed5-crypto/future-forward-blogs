# Best Free Website Makers in 2026: Options Compared

The best free site maker depends on your website: consider Wix for a visual business-site starting point, WordPress.com for regular articles, and Google Sites for straightforward information pages. Webflow and Framer are also worth considering when you want to explore visual design, but check their publishing limits before committing.

## How to compare these website makers

### Start with the job your website needs to do

A photographer needs to display work and receive enquiries. A local repair business needs clear service information and contact details, while a publisher needs a manageable way to add and organize articles.

Write down your main visitor action before choosing a template. That action gives you a practical way to judge whether a builder suits your site.

The recommendations here are editorial judgments based on official product documentation, rather than results from hands-on tests. Plan details were checked on November 15, 2026, and can change after that date.

### Compare your essential pages

Prepare a homepage, an About page, a service or work page, and a Contact page. If articles are central to the project, include a sample post with headings, a table, an image, and related links.

Use your actual text when exploring a builder. A layout that works with a short sample headline may need adjustments when you add your business name or a longer service description.

For an initial business website, clarity matters more than decoration. Visitors should quickly understand what you offer and how to take the next step.

### Separate free publishing from paid upgrades

A free account, a free trial, and a free published website are different offers. Check whether you can keep the site online without subscribing and which address the free version uses.

Wix, WordPress.com, Webflow, and Framer all document free publishing options with restrictions. Their free addresses and custom-domain requirements are covered below, so you can see where an upgrade becomes relevant.

Avoid paying for hypothetical features at the start. Identify the requirement that would justify an upgrade, such as using your business domain or adding a specific integration.

## Each builder: pros, cons, limits

### Wix: a visual starting point for a small business

Wix lets you create and publish a free website, with templates and design tools included. Its free offering is a sensible option to investigate when you want to assemble a small business site before paying for a subscription. See [Wix’s explanation of its free service](https://www.wix.com/blog/is-wix-free).

Start with a clear service description, relevant photographs, and one main contact action. Avoid adding a large collection of sections simply because a template includes them.

**Pros:** You can work on a live first version without buying a plan immediately. That makes it useful for organizing your content and deciding what the business website actually needs.

**Cons:** Free Wix sites use a Wix address and display Wix advertising. Connecting your own domain and removing Wix ads require an upgrade, according to [Wix’s upgrade guide](https://support.wix.com/en/article/upgrading-your-site-to-premium-3066683).

**Limits:** Wix documents up to 500 MB of storage for free sites. Its official pages show inconsistent bandwidth figures, so this comparison avoids giving a single guaranteed allowance; check the allowance shown for your account before planning around it. See [Wix’s storage and bandwidth guidance](https://support.wix.com/en/article/about-storage-and-bandwidth).

For an AdSense website, the free plan is an important limitation. Wix’s own AdSense integration requires a connected domain and an upgraded Premium or Studio plan. [Wix AdSense requirements](https://support.wix.com/en/article/about-the-wix-monetize-with-adsense-app).

**Our recommendation:** Consider Wix for developing a service-site first version. Budget for an upgrade if your business identity or advertising plan depends on capabilities outside the free offering.

### WordPress.com: a practical choice for regular articles

WordPress.com’s free plan includes hosting, unlimited pages and posts, and 1 GB of media storage. These documented features make it our suggested starting point among this shortlist for someone learning to maintain an article-led website. [WordPress.com plan features](https://wordpress.com/support/plan-features/).

Build a sample article before spending time on the homepage. Add meaningful subheadings, an image, and links, then consider whether that process fits how you expect to publish each week.

**Pros:** Pages and posts are not limited to a small fixed count on the free plan. That removes one possible obstacle when your main task is steadily adding useful written content.

**Cons:** The free version uses a WordPress.com address and displays platform advertising. A paid plan is needed to use your custom domain and remove those ads. [WordPress.com’s free-versus-paid distinctions](https://wordpress.com/support/plan-features/personal-plan/).

**Limits:** Free publishing does not mean unrestricted customization. Plugin installation is included in paid plans rather than the free plan, so check your intended integrations before deciding. [WordPress.com plan comparison](https://wordpress.com/support/plan-features/).

Portability is another consideration. WordPress.com provides a content export, but that export is not a complete copy of the theme, design settings, plugins, and every aspect of the site. [WordPress.com export documentation](https://wordpress.com/support/export/).

**Our recommendation:** Choose it for learning a publishing routine or starting an article archive. Before committing to ad income, distinguish the provider’s ads from your own monetization: its WordAds program requires Premium or above, and third-party ad services have their own implementation requirements. [WordPress.com monetization guide](https://wordpress.com/support/monetize-your-site/).

### Google Sites: straightforward information pages

Google Sites supports creating websites for personal or business use. Google documents adding text and pages, publishing the site, and choosing who can see it. [Google Sites creation guide](https://support.google.com/sites/answer/98081?hl=en) and [publishing guide](https://support.google.com/sites/answer/6372880?hl=en).

Our suggested use case is a small information site: a project overview, a resource collection, or basic service information. Begin with the information people need rather than trying to reproduce a complex marketing site.

**Pros:** Google Sites supports inserting Google files and embedding other content. This is useful to consider when the website’s purpose is presenting existing documents, forms, or other resources. [Google Sites embedding guide](https://support.google.com/sites/answer/90569?lev=index).

**Cons:** Embedded content can have separate access permissions. A page being public does not mean every inserted file is visible to visitors, so check the complete site while logged out.

**Limits:** A custom domain is supported, but only the site owner can connect it. Work or school accounts may need administrator involvement, and Google notes limitations for features that depend on Google Account authentication when using a custom domain. [Google Sites custom domains](https://support.google.com/sites/answer/9068867?hl=en).

**Our recommendation:** Consider Google Sites when a clear information page is the goal. For a publication built around advertising or specialized content workflows, establish those requirements separately rather than assuming an embed can supply them.

### Webflow: useful for learning visual design

Webflow’s free Starter Site plan includes a Webflow.io address, two static pages, 1 GB of bandwidth, and 50 form submissions. A custom domain requires a paid Site plan. [Webflow pricing and limits](https://webflow.com/pricing).

Those limits affect the kind of project you should start. A two-page experiment can fit, while a conventional four-page business site exceeds the free static-page allowance.

**Pros:** The free option gives you a way to explore the design environment before buying hosting for a custom-domain site. Use a compact project so you can judge the workflow without designing pages the free plan cannot publish.

**Cons:** Webflow separates Site plans from Workspace plans. Treat these as different decisions: paying for workspace capabilities does not automatically mean your website has the hosting plan required for its domain.

**Limits:** Code export is a paid Workspace capability, not part of the free Starter Workspace. Webflow also states that dynamic CMS content is not included in the HTML and CSS export. [Webflow Workspace comparison](https://webflow.com/pricing).

**Our recommendation:** Consider Webflow if learning layout control is part of the goal. For a beginner who simply needs a larger business website online, compare the required paid arrangement before investing heavily in the build.

### Framer: a visual option for a personal project

Framer lists a $0 Free plan with a free Framer domain and 1 GB bandwidth. It requires a paid plan to connect your own domain. [Framer pricing](https://www.framer.com/pricing).

That makes it an option for exploring a portfolio idea or other small personal project. Use the free stage to judge whether you can maintain the design yourself.

**Pros:** You can begin without a paid site subscription. For a visual project, focus on how clearly the layout presents your actual work rather than adding effects without a purpose.

**Cons:** Published free sites display a “Made in Framer” banner, according to [Framer’s plan guidance](https://www.framer.com/help/articles/best-use-cases-for-each-framer-plan/). Decide whether that branding is acceptable for the project before sharing the address widely.

**Limits:** Framer does not offer HTML export for self-hosting. Moving a project to another Framer user is different from moving its code to another hosting provider. [Framer’s export explanation](https://www.framer.com/help/articles/can-i-export-my-website-to-html-and-self-host-it/).

**Our recommendation:** Consider it for a visual first version when the free domain and branding are acceptable. Keep original text and images separately so your source material remains organized regardless of the platform.

### What about AI website builders?

Wix explicitly offers an AI website builder you can start using free. It is our suggested first option to investigate if you want an AI-generated starting layout within this shortlist, rather than a claim that it wins an output-quality test. [Wix AI website builder](https://www.wix.com/ai-website-builder).

Webflow and Framer also list AI capabilities in their offerings. However, an AI tool being included does not mean every AI function is unlimited or every publishing feature is free; both providers describe plan-dependent access or usage allowances. [Webflow plans](https://webflow.com/pricing), [Framer plans](https://www.framer.com/pricing).

Give the tool your real page requirements and approved text. Review the result for invented services, unsupported promises, and incorrect contact information before treating it as website copy.

The best free AI website builder for your situation should reduce the total work needed to produce an accurate site. A polished preview is only the beginning if it takes substantial effort to correct.

## Which one fits your site

### For a small service business

Start by investigating Wix if visual page creation is your priority. Consider Google Sites if you mainly need a straightforward information resource and its documented publishing model fits the project.

For either route, build around an enquiry: explain the service, show relevant information, and provide a working contact method. Test that journey before spending time on extra visual sections.

A custom business address may change the choice. Wix requires a paid plan for domain connection, while Google Sites supports connecting a domain you own, subject to its account and setup requirements.

### For a blog or practical-guide website

WordPress.com is our suggested starting point because its free plan includes unlimited posts and pages. Keep its storage, customization, and monetization limits in mind as the archive grows.

Plan the repeated work before choosing a theme. You should be comfortable adding articles, correcting older information, and connecting related guides without treating every update as a redesign.

Do not choose solely because the first homepage looks good. An article-led site needs a publishing process you can sustain.

### For a portfolio or learning project

Consider Framer for a visual personal project, or Webflow if you want to learn its design workflow within the two-static-page constraint. Choose according to the work you want to maintain, rather than assuming either tool is universally easier.

Use real images and descriptive captions. Ask someone unfamiliar with the site to find your work and contact details, then simplify anything that causes confusion.

### For a custom domain and future growth

Among these options, Google Sites is worth investigating when connecting an already-owned domain without a separate builder upgrade is important. Domain registration is still a separate expense, and work or school accounts can involve administrator controls.

Wix, WordPress.com, Webflow, and Framer require a paid plan for their custom-domain routes. Compare the relevant subscription and renewal terms before deciding that a free starting point is the cheapest long-term choice.

### Make a decision you can maintain

Ask the future owner to change a paragraph, replace an image, and update a navigation label. If the process is confusing, improve the instructions or reconsider the tool before the site becomes larger.

Keep original content organized outside the editor and decide who is responsible for updates. Clear ownership matters as much as a clear homepage.

The best free site maker is the one that fits your current publishing job and gives you an acceptable next step when your needs change. Start small, work within the documented limits, and upgrade when you can identify the problem the extra spending solves.

## FAQ

### What is the best free site maker for a small business?

Wix is our suggested starting point for a visual business-site build, while Google Sites is worth considering for straightforward information pages. Choose based on your domain requirements, contact workflow, and the limits explained above.

### Can I get a free website builder with a custom domain?

Google Sites supports connecting a domain you own without a separate Sites builder upgrade, although registration and renewal remain separate. Wix, WordPress.com, Webflow, and Framer require a paid plan for custom-domain use, as their documentation linked above explains.

### What is the best free AI website builder in 2026?

Wix is a useful first option to investigate because its official AI builder page offers a free starting point. This is a recommendation based on documented availability, and the free site's domain and advertising restrictions still apply.

### Can I publish a free website without knowing how to code?

Yes: Wix offers website-building tools, WordPress.com provides an editing interface, and Google Sites documents creating and publishing pages without a coding workflow. Try a small page first and choose the interface you can update confidently.
