# How to Use AI in Marketing: Practical Examples

To learn how to use ai in marketing, start with one clear task: turn approved information into a draft, prepare message variations, or summarise campaign data for review. Give the tool your audience, goal, and verified facts, then check the result before publishing, sending, or changing a campaign.

## Where AI helps: how to use AI in marketing

A useful starting point is work you can describe clearly and judge afterward. Preparing an outline from supplied notes is easier to evaluate than asking a tool to invent an entire marketing strategy without business context.

Use the examples below as proposed workflows, not promised performance improvements. The business scenarios and prompts are illustrative; no campaign results or personal tool tests are claimed.

### Separate creating from automating

Generative AI can be used to propose text or images, while other AI applications support decisions within marketing systems. For example, Google describes [Smart Bidding](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14697340?hl=en) as automated bidding that uses Google AI to optimise for conversions or conversion value in ad auctions.

That is a different job from drafting an advert. Identify which part of the work you want help with before selecting software or enabling automation.

Digital marketing automation also includes straightforward rules, such as a scheduled reminder or a message triggered by a defined action. You do not need an AI decision for every repeated task.

### Create a brief before choosing a tool

Write down the audience, offer, channel, and desired action. Add approved facts, wording that must remain unchanged, and anything the draft must not claim.

Imagine a fictional garden maintenance company promoting seasonal garden tidy-ups. Its brief could specify local homeowners, an enquiry form as the next step, and a requirement to avoid promising availability before the team checks its schedule.

Keep that brief as your reference throughout the work. If the output adds an unsupported discount or guarantee, reject the addition even if it sounds persuasive.

### Start with a manageable pilot

Choose one task you already perform regularly, such as drafting a newsletter introduction. Save the original inputs, the generated suggestion, and the version you approve.

Record the editing effort and any errors you needed to correct. Use those observations to decide whether the workflow is worth repeating, rather than assuming AI automatically saves time.

## Content

### Turn customer questions into a useful outline

Start with questions your business actually receives, using anonymised notes for the initial exercise. Group them around one reader problem instead of asking for an unrelated list of popular topics.

For the fictional garden company, questions might concern what a tidy-up includes, how customers should prepare, and how to request a quote. Ask for an outline that answers those questions in a sensible order.

Example prompt:

> Create an outline for homeowners considering a garden tidy-up. Use only these supplied questions and service notes. Put the practical answer first, identify missing information, and do not invent prices, availability, or guarantees.

Check whether each proposed heading serves the reader. Remove sections that repeat the same point or require expertise you cannot supply.

### Draft from an approved fact sheet

Prepare a short source document describing the service, exclusions, process, and next step. Ask the assistant to use that document as the basis for a first draft.

Work section by section so you can correct the direction early. An entire polished article is harder to review when the underlying premise is wrong.

Example prompt:

> Draft the section explaining what this service includes, using the approved notes below. Write for a beginner, keep paragraphs short, and flag anything that needs confirmation. Do not add customer stories or performance claims.

Add your own useful explanations and examples during editing. If your experience would strengthen a passage, supply a real observation rather than asking the tool to invent one.

### Repurpose an approved article

Once a longer article is checked, use it as the source for a newsletter introduction or short social post. State which details must stay and what action the shorter version should encourage.

For example, turn a guide about preparing for a garden visit into a short checklist. Compare the checklist with the original so an important preparation step is not lost.

Do not ask the tool to create a fresh factual explanation every time you change channels. Reuse the approved information and adapt its presentation.

### Keep SEO focused on the reader

Choose the question your page should answer before drafting. Our [keyword research guide](https://futureforwardstudios.com/how-to-find-keywords-for-seo/) explains how to connect topic ideas with search intent.

Google's [generative AI guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content) emphasises accuracy, quality, and relevance. It also warns that generating many pages without adding user value may violate its scaled content abuse policy.

Use AI to improve a useful page rather than multiply near-identical pages. Check whether the final content answers the question with enough practical detail for the intended reader.

## Email and ads

### Draft emails around one clear action

Choose what the reader should do after reading the email. Then provide the offer, audience, relevant conditions, and approved destination link.

Mailchimp documents a [Write with AI feature](https://mailchimp.com/help/write-with-ai/) for content creation. Check its current availability and account requirements before deciding whether it fits your existing email setup.

You can also evaluate a writing assistant using a fictional campaign brief. Ask for a draft and alternatives, then place the approved wording in your usual email workflow.

Example prompt:

> Draft a short email using these approved service details. Ask readers to request a quote through the supplied link. Keep availability conditional, include the stated exclusions, and suggest subject lines that accurately describe the message.

Review the subject line and body together. A subject line should not imply a guaranteed appointment if the body only offers a chance to enquire.

### Use variations to test a specific idea

Prepare two versions around a clear difference, such as a practical benefit versus an explanation of the process. Keep the offer and destination consistent so you understand what you are comparing.

Define the outcome before running the test. For the fictional garden company, the question might concern completed quote requests rather than which message sounds more exciting.

Treat a proposed variant as a hypothesis. It is not a winning message until your own appropriately designed comparison supports that conclusion.

### Prepare ad copy with explicit boundaries

For ai marketing campaigns, give the assistant the same approved offer information used on the landing page. Ask for variations that emphasise different supported benefits without changing the underlying promise.

Example prompt:

> Propose three ad angles for this service: convenience, preparation guidance, and clarity about the process. Use only the facts supplied. Do not add rankings, savings, guarantees, or customer reviews, and flag wording that needs checking against the ad platform's current limits.

Check the selected platform's current requirements before uploading the copy. Review the final asset in context, including its destination page and any automatically combined elements.

### Review generated visuals and automated decisions

Google Ads offers generative image tools, and its [guidance on generated images](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14150986?hl=en) tells advertisers to review assets for accuracy and misleading content before publication. Apply that review to what the image suggests, not only the wording beside it.

For example, a garden-service image should not imply that specialised equipment or a particular result is included when it is not. Use an image that matches the actual service and check details before approving it.

If you use automated bidding, confirm the conversion actions you want the platform to optimise toward. A completed quote request and a visit to an information page represent different business outcomes.

## Reporting

### Start with a question and a defined dataset

Ask what you need to understand, such as which campaigns generated enquiries during a stated period. Export the relevant report and include metric definitions, date ranges, and any tracking changes.

Remove information the analysis does not need and confirm your organisation's rules before sharing non-public data with an external tool. Aggregated campaign totals are often enough for an initial reporting exercise.

Tell the assistant to distinguish observations from explanations. A rise in enquiries is visible in the figures; the reason for that rise may require more evidence.

### Use a small example to check the arithmetic

Consider this fictional reporting exercise: a campaign spends £200 and records 10 enquiries in one period, then spends £300 and records 12 enquiries in another. The cost per enquiry is £20 and £25 respectively, calculated by dividing spend by enquiries.

The second period has more enquiries but a higher cost per enquiry. That describes the sample data without proving that the campaign has become more or less profitable.

Example prompt:

> Summarise this table using only the supplied figures. Show the formula for cost per enquiry, compare the periods, and separate observations from possible explanations. List missing information needed to judge lead quality or business value.

Check the calculations yourself in a spreadsheet. If the output recommends a budget change, identify what evidence supports that recommendation before acting.

### Turn the summary into a short action list

Ask for follow-up questions as well as a narrative. Useful questions might concern whether enquiry quality changed, whether the periods are comparable, and whether the tracking setup remained consistent.

Connect those questions with the people who can answer them. Your sales team may know which enquiries were suitable, while the campaign owner can check changes to targeting or creative.

Our [SEO report guide](https://futureforwardstudios.com/seo-report-generator/) offers a reporting structure for small sites. Adapt its focus on observations and next actions to the marketing channels you use.

### Keep interpretation separate from approval

Use the generated report as a draft for discussion. Have the responsible person review figures, explanations, and proposed actions before sharing it as a final business conclusion.

Keep the source report with the summary. That gives you a way to revisit the analysis if a number or interpretation is questioned later.

## Mistakes to avoid

### Asking for strategy without business context

A broad request for a complete marketing strategy leaves too many decisions undefined. Supply your customer, offer, available channels, and operational constraints before asking for ideas.

Choose a problem you can evaluate, then expand only when the first workflow is useful. This keeps AI for marketers connected to real work rather than a growing list of disconnected experiments.

### Publishing claims you have not checked

Review every added fact, testimonial, price, and promise. If the source does not support it, remove it or obtain the information before publication.

Keep real experience separate from fictional examples. Never turn an illustrative customer scenario into a claimed case study or measured result.

### Automating a process nobody owns

Assign someone to approve drafts, maintain reference information, and review outcomes. Define when an automated workflow should pause for human attention.

Keep the first implementation small enough to inspect. A reliable content brief and review routine should come before connecting several tools into an unsupervised publishing process.

### Changing too many things at once

If you introduce a new offer, rewrite the message, change the audience, and alter the landing page together, keep a record of those changes. Do not attribute the outcome to AI-generated wording alone when several parts of the campaign changed.

For the fictional garden company, a manageable first exercise would be drafting one email from an already approved offer. Review its quality before adding ad generation or reporting automation to the same project.

Write down the decision you want the exercise to support. You might be deciding whether the drafting process needs less correction or whether a particular message deserves a separate campaign test.

Keep those questions distinct. A faster draft and a more effective campaign are different outcomes, and each needs its own evidence.

### Choosing volume over useful results

Do not judge the workflow only by the number of drafts produced. Review how much usable work reaches publication and whether the content serves the intended reader.

Keep a record of corrections, accepted edits, and campaign observations. Use those records to decide what to improve and which subscriptions remain worthwhile.

Learning how to use ai in marketing means matching a clear task with reliable inputs, then checking the output against your business goal. Start with one workflow, preserve the facts, and expand when your own evidence justifies the next step.

## FAQ

### How can a small business start using AI in marketing?

Choose one repeatable task, such as outlining an article or drafting a newsletter from approved notes. Define the audience and goal, then review the result before expanding the workflow.

### What are practical examples of AI in marketing?

Examples include proposing content outlines, preparing email variations, drafting ad concepts, and summarising campaign reports. These are tasks to evaluate against your own requirements, not guarantees of better campaign performance.

### Can AI run marketing campaigns automatically?

Some platforms automate particular decisions, such as Google Ads Smart Bidding for conversions or conversion value. You still need to choose suitable goals, check the setup, and review the business outcomes.

### How do I measure whether AI is helping my marketing?

Track the work completed, editing effort, corrections, and relevant campaign outcomes against a defined starting point. Separate time spent producing drafts from evidence that the finished marketing supports your goals.
