# Online Marketing Tools Every Small Business Should Know

Start with online marketing tools that support a clear customer journey: help people find your business, explain your offer, capture enquiries, and track what happens next. Google Search Console, Google Business Profile, Brevo, Buffer, Canva, Google Analytics, and HubSpot are useful candidates to evaluate, but you do not need to adopt all seven at once.

## Tools by marketing channel

A marketing channel is a route through which you reach customers, such as search, email, or social media. A tool supports the work within or across those routes; buying the software does not decide which audience you should reach.

Choose a starting channel based on how customers find and evaluate your business. Then add the tools needed to publish, respond, and measure the work you can maintain.

The recommendations below use current official product information rather than hands-on performance rankings. Suggested uses are editorial judgments, not promises of traffic, leads, or sales.

### Search: Google Search Console

Google describes [Search Console](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/9128668?hl=en) as a free service for monitoring, maintaining, and troubleshooting a site's presence in Google Search. It is a sensible starting point when your business publishes pages intended to answer customer searches.

Use it with a specific question, such as whether an important service page is appearing for relevant searches. Keep the enquiry or sale you want that page to support in view when reviewing visibility.

Avoid treating every increase in impressions as a completed business outcome. Decide whether the page explains the offer clearly and gives visitors a suitable next step.

A practical routine is to choose one important page, review its search information, and decide whether the content needs improvement. Our [SEO audit checklist](https://futureforwardstudios.com/seo-audit-checklist/) can help you turn that review into a useful action list.

**Evaluate it when:** your website is part of your search marketing plan.

**Keep separate:** search visibility, website activity, and actual customer outcomes. They answer related questions, but they are not interchangeable measurements.

### Local discovery: Google Business Profile

A [Google Business Profile](https://business.google.com/us/business-profile/) lets an eligible business manage its presence on Search and Maps. Google's [eligibility guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) covers businesses with a location customers can visit or those that travel to customers.

For a fictional plumbing business, the first task would be accurate service information and contact details. Confirm eligibility and the correct representation of the business before creating or changing a listing.

Assign someone to keep hours and other business information current. A profile should reflect how the business actually operates rather than an idealised version of its availability.

Also plan who will review and respond to customer feedback. Keep responses factual, helpful, and appropriate for a public conversation.

**Evaluate it when:** local discovery matters and your business meets the requirements.

**Keep separate:** listing management and paid advertising. A free profile does not establish a budget or strategy for an advertising campaign.

### Email: Brevo

Brevo's [plan documentation](https://help.brevo.com/hc/en-us/articles/208589409-About-Brevo-s-pricing-plans) lists email campaigns, an email editor, and basic reporting in Free. Its published allowance is 300 sends daily, with unused sends not carried into the next day.

That makes the sending schedule a practical buying consideration. A contact storage allowance is not the same as permission or capacity to email everyone at once.

Before evaluating an email tool, define who the message is for and why they should receive it. Prepare a small sample campaign with one clear next action and review the complete message before sending.

For a fictional workshop business, that action might be viewing an upcoming class page. Check the subject line, dates, destination link, and any booking conditions together.

**Evaluate it when:** email is a channel you can maintain and the selected plan fits your sending needs.

**Keep separate:** list size, sending allowance, and required features. Compare all three before deciding that a free account is enough.

### Social publishing: Buffer

Buffer's [Free plan](https://buffer.com/pricing) supports up to three channels, with 10 scheduled posts per channel. Its [queue-limit explanation](https://support.buffer.com/en-us/articles/how-many-posts-can-i-schedule-in-advance-Kmy2IEecqm) clarifies that this concerns posts scheduled at a time, rather than a total lifetime allowance.

Use a scheduler when organising posts is a real problem. First decide which platforms deserve your attention and what useful information you will publish there.

A fictional bakery might prepare a small set of approved posts about ordering, collection, and seasonal products. Each post should point to accurate information rather than repeat a generic promotional message.

Check the final preview and destination for every platform you use. Also decide who will monitor replies after publication; scheduling content does not replace customer interaction.

**Evaluate it when:** you have a manageable social plan and need help maintaining the schedule.

**Keep separate:** publishing consistently and proving that social activity generates useful enquiries or sales.

### Creative work: Canva

Canva provides [free templates](https://www.canva.com/templates/) and separates Free from paid offerings on its [pricing page](https://www.canva.com/pricing/). It is worth evaluating when simple graphics are a recurring part of your marketing work.

Start with one approved layout for a specific purpose, such as a workshop announcement. Replace the sample information with accurate dates, a clear offer, and a readable next step.

Check the assets and functions used in the finished design. Do not assume every element shown during browsing belongs to the free offering or has identical usage conditions.

Keep a small collection of approved designs rather than changing the visual style with every post. Ask whether the graphic remains readable at the size customers will actually see.

**Evaluate it when:** you regularly need simple marketing visuals and can define what a finished asset must communicate.

**Keep separate:** attractive design and clear communication. A polished graphic still needs correct information and a useful purpose.

### Measurement: Google Analytics

Google's [Analytics overview](https://marketingplatform.google.com/about/analytics/) describes free tools for understanding customer activity across websites and apps. Consider it when you have a specific measurement question and someone responsible for setup and interpretation.

Define the website actions that matter before building a dashboard. For a service company, a completed enquiry and a visit to an article represent different stages of the customer journey.

Have the implementation checked against those definitions. Review which events are being recorded and whether the reports answer the question you intended to ask.

Keep a record of major website and tracking changes. That context helps when comparing periods and deciding whether an apparent change needs investigation.

**Evaluate it when:** you need website measurement beyond search visibility alone.

**Keep separate:** recorded website actions and confirmed business results. Connect reporting with the people who handle enquiries and sales.

### Enquiry management: HubSpot CRM

HubSpot's [CRM page](https://www.hubspot.com/products/crm) lists contact, deal, and task management alongside its free allowance. A CRM supports the handoff after someone responds to your marketing, rather than acting as a customer acquisition channel itself.

For a small team, begin with an owner and a next action for each active enquiry. Decide who records the conversation and when the opportunity should be marked complete or closed.

Use fictional records during initial evaluation. Follow one enquiry from the website to a reply, a scheduled conversation, and a clear outcome.

Confirm any website or email connection in the exact plan you intend to use. A product's broader capability list does not establish what your selected package includes.

**Evaluate it when:** follow-ups or shared customer information are becoming difficult to manage.

**Keep separate:** receiving enquiries and handling them well. Our [small-business CRM guide](https://futureforwardstudios.com/best-crm-for-small-businesses/) provides alternatives when this is your main problem.

## Free vs paid

### Compare allowances in the right units

List the limits relevant to each task: daily email sends, queued posts, users, contacts, or specific assets and functions. Do not compare free plans only by the fact that their starting price is zero.

Write down your normal workload before opening accounts. Then check whether the allowance fits how the team actually works, including occasional busy periods.

For example, a low daily email volume and a large single announcement require different evaluations. Base the decision on the intended send rather than the number of contacts you could theoretically store.

### Include setup and maintenance in the cost

A free subscription can still require time to configure, maintain, and review. Assign those responsibilities before deciding to add another tool.

Keep a short inventory showing the account owner, purpose, connected systems, and next review date. Record which person can retrieve the information if someone leaves the business.

For a paid option, compare the first payment with the annual total. Check billing terms, required seats, add-ons, and the package containing your essential feature.

### Upgrade for a defined requirement

A useful upgrade reason is concrete: the current queue does not fit the publishing routine, or the required team workflow is unavailable. Describe the limitation before comparing paid plans.

Ask whether a simpler process would solve the problem first. If the business only needs two posts approved each week, an elaborate workflow may add more administration than value.

Keep a record of why the upgrade was chosen. Revisit that reason when you review subscriptions and remove tools that no longer support useful work.

## Starter stack: choosing online marketing tools

### Begin with the customer journey

Write a short route from discovery to outcome: a person finds a service page, requests information, receives a reply, and decides whether to proceed. Choose tools to support each necessary step.

For a fictional local service business, a reasonable starting evaluation is a clear website, Search Console, and an eligible Business Profile. Add a consistent follow-up process before expanding into more marketing channels.

Our [business website guide](https://futureforwardstudios.com/free-business-website/) can help you establish the pages and contact journey. Keep the offer and next action understandable before investing in wider promotion.

### Add one active communication channel

Choose email or a social platform based on your customers and your capacity to publish useful material. Evaluate Brevo for the email workflow or Buffer for a small social schedule.

Use Canva only if preparing visuals is part of that plan. A tool earns its place when it helps complete a required task, rather than because it is included in someone else's recommended stack.

Prepare one small campaign and follow the complete journey yourself. Inspect the message, destination page, form, notification, and follow-up responsibility before launch.

### Add measurement and shared records deliberately

Introduce Analytics when you can name the website question it should help answer. Introduce a CRM when the team needs a shared way to maintain customer conversations and next actions.

Review the connections between tools before relying on them. Ask what information moves, in which direction, and who investigates a failed transfer.

Start with a limited set of records and actions. Improve that workflow before adding automation around a process the team has not agreed on.

### Run a small acceptance check

Before depending on the stack, write down what success looks like for one complete journey. A fictional workshop business might require that a newsletter link opens the right class page, a form records the requested date, and an employee receives enough information to respond.

Use a clearly labelled test enquiry and inspect every handoff. Confirm who owns the reply and where the outcome will be recorded, then remove the test from any customer-facing reports where appropriate.

Check the same journey on a phone as well as a larger screen. Read the offer, follow the link, and make sure the next action remains understandable without extra explanation.

Record any failures in a short list with an owner and a next step. Fix those gaps before increasing the volume of promotion, since more publishing will not resolve a broken destination or an unassigned enquiry.

### Establish a repeatable review routine

Review what was published, which useful responses arrived, and what needs attention next. Use the same definitions each time so the discussion stays connected to business outcomes.

Choose one improvement for the next cycle, such as clarifying a service page or fixing an enquiry handoff. Do not change every channel at once and then attribute the result to one tool.

The right online marketing tools support a customer journey your business can maintain. Start small, check the limits, and expand when a clear requirement justifies the extra work or cost.

## FAQ

### What online marketing tools should a small business start with?

Start with tools that support your main customer journey, such as Search Console for a search-focused website and Business Profile for an eligible local business. Add email, social scheduling, design, and shared customer records only where the workflow requires them.

### Can I market a small business using free tools?

Several tools in this guide offer free access, including Search Console, eligible Business Profiles, and limited plans from Brevo, Buffer, and HubSpot. Check the allowances and your capacity to maintain the work before deciding that a free setup covers your needs.

### How many marketing channels should I use?

Start with the channels you can support with relevant content and reliable follow-up. Expand when you have a clear audience, a reason to use the additional channel, and someone responsible for the work.

### When should I pay for a marketing tool?

Pay when a specific requirement exceeds your current plan and the appropriate upgrade fits your budget. Include implementation, maintenance, and the full billing commitment in that decision.
