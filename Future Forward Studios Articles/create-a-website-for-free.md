# How to Create a Website for Free in 2026 (Step by Step)

To create a website for free, choose a platform with a suitable free publishing option, add your essential pages, and check the live site before sharing it. Start with one purpose and a simple design; verify the platform’s current limits before investing time in the build.

## What you need before you start

### Decide what the website should do

Write down one action you want visitors to take: view your work, read your articles, or ask about a service. Use that action to decide which pages and features belong in the first version.

For example, a freelance photographer could start with a portfolio and an enquiry link. A beginner publishing practical guides needs an article index and a readable post layout instead.

### Prepare your words and images

Draft a short explanation of what you offer and who it helps. Collect your logo, photographs you can use, and the contact details you want to publish.

For a small business, prepare a homepage, a service or work page, an About page, and a Contact page. Describe your actual work rather than filling the site with broad claims such as “the best service.”

As a starting point, plan four pages: a homepage, a work or service page, an About page, and a Contact page. Expect the About and service pages to take the longest to prepare, because they need real details that only you can write.

### List your essential requirements

Separate what you need now from what can wait. A contact method might be essential, while a booking calendar or animated homepage could be optional.

If advertising is part of your plan, investigate that before choosing a platform. Google AdSense requires you to own the site and control its HTML, and it accepts a plain domain rather than a free platform subdomain (subdomains are allowed only on certain host-partner platforms). Wix says sites on a free wixsite.com address cannot use AdSense, and WordPress.com says it cannot provide WordAds on the default free address. Details are in the platform sections below. Rules change, so confirm them in each provider's help pages before you build around advertising.

### Compare a real task

Try making the same sample homepage in your shortlisted tools. Add a heading, an image, a paragraph, and a contact link, then inspect the result on your phone.

Notice how easily you can change navigation, correct a typo, and add another page. These ordinary tasks matter more than a template that looks impressive before you replace its sample content.

### Distinguish a free plan from a trial

Check whether the website can remain published without payment and whether you must enter payment details. For example, Wix says its free plan has no time limit and needs no credit card, and WordPress.com lists its free plan with no expiration date. A paid trial or upgrade is different: read the checkout page for the renewal price and cancellation terms. Wix notes that if a paid plan lapses, the site reverts to a free Wix address and a connected custom domain is disconnected.

Save the relevant plan details and the date you checked them. That gives you a record if the offer changes while you are building.

## Step-by-step setup: create a website for free

### Step 1: Choose one platform

Compare your essential requirements with the verified plan details. Eliminate options that cannot support your main visitor action, even if you prefer their templates.

Pick one candidate for a small first version rather than rebuilding the entire site in several tools. Keep copies of your written content outside the platform so revisions are easy to reuse.

### Step 2: Create the account

Use an email address you control and protect the account with the security options available. Record who owns the account, especially if someone else helps build the website.

Look in the platform's account security settings for two-step verification and account recovery options before you build, and turn them on. Google Sites and GitHub Pages use your Google or GitHub account, so their security settings apply.

Avoid sharing one password among several people. Check this before inviting anyone. Google Sites lets the owner decide who can edit and supports real-time co-editing, though only the owner can connect a custom domain. Wix lists collaborator counts only for paid plans, so its free plan's allowance is not documented there.

### Step 3: Start with a simple template

Choose a layout that fits your content: a portfolio, an article-led site, or a service website. Replace the sample headline early so you can judge the design using your real words.

Remove sections you cannot fill honestly. An empty testimonial carousel or invented customer count makes the site harder to trust.

Pick a template by looking at its structure, not its sample photos. Choose one whose layout already has the sections you need, so you spend your time replacing words rather than rebuilding the page.

### Step 4: Build the homepage

Use the first screen to explain what the site offers and what visitors can do next. A clear statement such as “Product photography for independent online shops” gives more direction than “Welcome to our website.”

Follow it with relevant work, a brief explanation, and one main action. If the action is an enquiry, make the link easy to find.

Keep your first homepage focused. You can add sections later when you have something useful to put in them.

### Step 5: Add the supporting pages

Use the About page to explain who is behind the website and why the subject matters to you. Include real experience without adding credentials or achievements you cannot support.

On a service page, explain the work, who it suits, and what happens after an enquiry. On a Contact page, provide a working method and only the details you are comfortable making public.

Check any privacy information against the data your site actually collects. If your site uses forms, analytics, or advertising, publish a privacy policy that says what you collect and why. Laws such as the EU and UK GDPR and the California CCPA may apply depending on where your visitors live, and ad programs have their own disclosure rules. Note that GitHub says it logs visitors' IP addresses for sites on GitHub Pages. This is general information, not legal advice.

### Step 6: Connect the navigation

Choose labels visitors can understand without guessing. “Services,” “About,” and “Contact” are easier to interpret than creative labels with unclear destinations.

Link related pages where the connection helps the reader. Use descriptive wording such as “view our product photography work” rather than repeating “click here.”

For Future Forward Studios, internal links should use `https://futureforwardstudios.com` followed by the relevant page path. Add links only after confirming those destination pages exist.

### Step 7: Set page titles and headings

Give each page a title that describes its purpose and a main heading that matches the content. Write a short page summary where the editor supports one.

This varies by platform and plan, and the help pages we checked do not confirm it for every free plan, so test it in the editor before committing: look for each page's title, description, address, and image description settings. On GitHub Pages you edit these directly in your HTML files.

Use related words naturally instead of inserting awkward phrases from a keyword list. Visitors should understand the page without noticing an effort to repeat search terms.

### Step 8: Review the mobile version

Open each page on a phone and read it from beginning to end. Look for tiny text, cropped images, crowded buttons, and headings that break awkwardly.

Check menus and contact actions with the same care as the homepage. A polished first screen is not enough if the next step fails.

Common problems on a phone include long headings that wrap awkwardly, images that crop the wrong part, and buttons placed too close together. Fix them by shortening the heading, choosing a better image crop, or adding space around the button, then check the page again.

### Step 9: Test before sharing

Follow every menu link and button. If there is a form, submit a test message and confirm that it reaches the intended destination.

Proofread names, contact details, and service information. Remove leftover sample content and check that images belong to the correct pages.

### Keep a simple launch checklist

Write down each page, its purpose, and the action you tested. Mark a page complete only after reviewing its text, checking its links, and opening it on a phone.

If someone helps you build the site, ask them to show you how to edit a paragraph and replace an image yourself. Practice those changes before handing over the final version so routine updates do not become a new project.

### Step 10: Publish and check again

Steps differ. In Google Sites, click Publish, choose a web address, and click Publish again, then set sharing to Restricted or Public. Free Wix sites publish to a wixsite.com address and free WordPress.com sites to a wordpress.com subdomain. GitHub Pages publishes from a repository to a github.io address (for a user site, owner.github.io). Check the visibility setting before you share the link.

After publishing, open the live address in a private browser window. Confirm that someone who is not logged into your account can see the intended pages.

Save the live URL and keep a short list of future improvements. Finish the working essentials before adding decorative features.

## What free plans do not include

Free plans have different boundaries, so do not assume one platform’s restrictions apply to another. Check the details that affect your intended site.

### Your preferred website address

Free Wix and WordPress.com sites use the platform's own address and do not include a custom domain. Google Sites supports a custom domain if you own one: it needs a subdomain such as www and DNS records, and only the site owner can connect it; buying a domain through Google requires a Google Workspace subscription. GitHub Pages supports a custom domain on top of its default github.io address.

Distinguish registering a domain from connecting it to a website. Check both costs and account ownership before buying either service.

### Full design and feature access

Wix free: about 500 MB storage and 1 GB bandwidth. WordPress.com free: 1 GB storage and no plugin installation. GitHub Pages: 1 GB site size, a soft 100 GB monthly traffic limit, and a soft limit of 10 builds an hour. Google's help pages do not list storage or page limits for Sites.

Look at the requirement you actually need. A restricted feature matters only if its absence prevents the site from doing its job.

### Unrestricted branding and monetization

Wix free sites show Wix branding and ads, and Wix says free wixsite.com sites cannot use AdSense. WordPress.com free sites show WordPress.com ads to visitors, and its WordAds program is not offered on the default free address. Google does not document AdSense support for Sites. GitHub Pages is not intended for running an online business or e-commerce. These rules change, so check each provider's current help page.

Do not assume that publishing a site means you can add any advertising service. Resolve this before planning a business around ad income.

### Easy migration

WordPress.com can export posts, pages, comments, categories, and tags as an XML file, but it does not include your theme, design customizations, plugins, or the media files themselves (only links), so download your images separately. The sources we checked do not describe a full export for free Wix or Google Sites pages, so plan to rebuild layouts and keep your own copies of text and images. GitHub Pages sites are plain files in your repository.

Keep your original text and images organized independently. That reduces the amount of material you need to recover if you later move.

## When to upgrade

Upgrade when a specific restriction interferes with the website’s purpose. Examples include needing an approved custom-domain setup, a required integration, or more control over the publishing workflow.

Write the problem down before comparing paid options. Then check whether the proposed plan actually solves it.

Prices change often, so read the checkout page before paying. As an example, Wix lists a Light plan from $17 a month on a yearly subscription, and WordPress.com says upgrading to Personal removes its ads. Check the renewal price, any required extras such as a domain, and the cancellation terms on the provider's own page.

Compare the complete cost rather than an introductory headline. Also consider whether switching platforms would require rebuilding pages or changing your process.

You can create a website for free to establish a useful first version and learn what visitors need. Publish clear content, test the journey, and spend money when you can name the benefit it buys.

## FAQ

### How do I create a website for free without coding?

Choose a platform whose current free offering supports the editing and publishing tasks you need. Wix and Google Sites are visual editors, and WordPress.com is a hosted website builder, so none require coding. GitHub Pages hosts static files from a repository, so it suits people comfortable with code. Free-plan limits are in the sections above.

### Can I use my own domain with a free website?

Check domain connection rules separately from domain registration. Free Wix and WordPress.com plans do not include one. Google Sites and GitHub Pages support a domain you already own, with DNS changes on your side. Registering a domain usually costs money, so check the price and renewal terms with the registrar.

### Can I put ads on a free website?

Check both the platform’s rules and the advertising service’s requirements before planning ad placement. AdSense requires you to own the site and control its HTML, an adult account holder, and original, high-quality content. Wix says a custom domain is required and free wixsite.com sites cannot use AdSense, and WordPress.com does not offer WordAds on its default free address. Approval is never guaranteed, so read Google's current AdSense eligibility help page.

### How long does creating a free website take?

Plan separate time for writing, building, and testing rather than treating publishing as the whole task. The time depends on how much is ready beforehand. Small sites often take longer to write than to build, so finish your text and images first and set aside separate time for testing on a phone.
