# WordPress Hosting: What to Look For and Who to Choose

Choose WordPress hosting by checking software compatibility, recovery options, support responsibilities, and the complete subscription cost. If your search started with “hosting wordpress,” focus on the plan behind that label: it should support your website’s actual workload and give you a clear way to recover when something goes wrong.

## Types of WordPress hosting — making sense of “hosting wordpress”

### Self-hosted WordPress and WordPress.com

WordPress.org provides the WordPress software, while a self-hosted website needs a separate hosting arrangement. WordPress.com includes hosting as part of its service, so these routes involve different purchasing and maintenance decisions. [WordPress.com and WordPress.org explained](https://wordpress.com/support/com-vs-org/).

If you are researching hosting on WordPress.org, do not assume the software download includes a hosting account. Decide which route you want before buying services that may duplicate something already included.

### Shared hosting

A shared plan puts multiple customer accounts on shared server infrastructure. A plan marketed for WordPress may provide compatible software and setup tools, but the exact maintenance service needs checking.

This can be a starting point for an ordinary business website or new publication. Read [our shared hosting explainer](https://futureforwardstudios.com/shared-hosting-explained/) to understand the resource-sharing arrangement before comparing offers.

### Managed WordPress hosting

Managed hosting describes work the provider takes on, rather than one universal technical specification. Ask which updates, backups, recovery tasks, and support activities are included in the specific offer.

DreamHost, for example, distinguishes its managed DreamPress service from other WordPress hosting options and explains that managed hosting does not replace all website maintenance. [DreamHost’s WordPress hosting guide](https://www.dreamhost.com/wordpress/).

Use that distinction when evaluating any provider. “Managed” should lead to a clear responsibility list, not an assumption that every problem becomes the host’s job.

### VPS and other configurations

A VPS provides a virtual server environment and can offer more configuration control than a standard shared account. Administration requirements depend on whether the service is managed or self-managed. [AWS VPS explanation](https://aws.amazon.com/what-is/vps/).

Consider this route when the application has a specific requirement that the simpler plans cannot meet. Buying more server control is not automatically useful if nobody is responsible for maintaining it.

## Features that matter

### Current software compatibility

WordPress currently recommends PHP 8.3 or greater, MariaDB 10.11 or greater or MySQL 8.4 or greater, and HTTPS support. Its requirements page also warns that older supported environments may use software that has reached end of life. [Official WordPress requirements](https://wordpress.org/about/requirements/).

Ask the host to confirm the environment for the plan you intend to buy. Also check any requirements imposed by your theme, plugins, or custom application code.

Keep the distinction between “it runs” and “it is an appropriate maintained setup.” A site loading successfully does not answer every compatibility and maintenance question.

### Backups you can actually restore

Ask how often backups run, how long they are retained, and how you request a restore. Confirm whether restoration has an extra charge and what gets overwritten.

For an active site, think about what happens between the backup and the restore. New enquiries, edits, or orders may require special handling rather than simply replacing everything with an older copy.

Keep an independent recovery copy where appropriate. Also document the procedure so recovery does not depend on one person remembering where a button is.

### A staging environment

Staging is a separate place to test changes before applying them to the live website. Check whether the selected plan includes it and how changes move between environments.

Do not assume pushing a test site live is harmless for every project. Ask how the process handles newer live data, especially if the website accepts orders or records customer activity.

### Clear support boundaries

Ask whether support covers hosting availability, WordPress installation issues, plugin conflicts, or custom development. Those are different kinds of work.

Use a concrete example when contacting a provider: “If an update breaks our contact form, what can your team investigate?” A specific question gives you more useful information than asking whether support is good.

### A maintenance process your team can follow

Ask the person responsible for the website to describe an ordinary update from start to finish. That includes preparing a recovery point, applying the change, checking important pages, and deciding what to do if something fails.

Use that sequence to evaluate the hosting interface. A tool only reduces work if your team knows where to find it and understands what its actions affect.

For example, a fictional small agency might appoint one person to approve plugin changes and another to review the enquiry form afterward. The host’s role should fit into that process without leaving a gap that everyone assumes somebody else will handle.

Keep a short record of significant changes. That gives a future investigation useful context without requiring anyone to reconstruct the site’s history from memory.

### Resource limits and billing terms

Compare storage, site allowances, and whatever workload metric the provider uses. Do not treat a suggested visitor figure as a universal promise for every plugin combination or page design.

Record the upfront payment, service period, renewal conditions, domain charges, and required extras. For broader budgeting guidance, read [our website cost comparison](https://futureforwardstudios.com/cheapest-website-builder/).

### Choose the provider after defining the job

For a fictional consultancy, the priorities might be readable service pages, reliable enquiries, and straightforward updates. For a busy publication, the priorities might include frequent publishing, change testing, and recovery from a broken update.

Use those requirements to evaluate the shortlist. The right host for a WordPress site is the one whose service and responsibilities fit the work, not simply the name appearing first in a recommendation list.

## Setup checklist

### 1. Decide who controls the accounts

Use an account owned by the business or website owner. Record who controls hosting, domain registration, billing, and administrator access.

If a freelancer sets things up, include access and maintenance instructions in the handover. Do not let essential ownership information disappear into a private conversation.

### 2. Confirm the plan and complete budget

Check software compatibility and the exact features included in the selected tier. Save the offer and checkout summary so the subscription period and renewal terms are clear.

Include required domain and email services separately. An included introductory period does not establish their long-term cost.

### 3. Install or migrate WordPress

Follow the chosen host’s current installation instructions. If moving an existing website, create the necessary backup and test the migration before treating it as complete.

Check pages, images, login, and any forms or transactions the business depends on. A successful import message is only one part of confirming that the site works.

### 4. Review settings and content

Confirm the intended public address and HTTPS setup. Check page titles, navigation, contact details, and the information visitors need before making a decision.

Remove sample pages and unsupported claims. Use [our business website walkthrough](https://futureforwardstudios.com/free-business-website/) if you need help organizing the essential pages.

### 5. Check backup and staging access

Find the relevant tools and read their instructions before an emergency. Confirm what is included in a backup and how you would restore it.

If staging is available, test a representative change there. Understand which content or settings would move to the live environment before using any publish or synchronization action.

### 6. Test the live visitor journey

Open the published website while logged out and use a phone as well as a larger screen. Submit an enquiry and confirm that it reaches the intended destination.

Check internal links and the main navigation. Record anything that still needs attention rather than assuming the homepage represents the whole website.

### Keep the old setup available during a move

If you are changing hosts, identify when each part of the website will switch over. Keep the material needed to recover and avoid deleting the previous account simply because the new homepage loads.

Check whether email or another service depends on settings associated with the domain. Record the intended changes before making them, so moving the website does not accidentally disrupt a separate business service.

After the move, repeat the checks that matter to visitors and administrators. Review contact delivery, editing access, images, and links before closing the migration work.

### 7. Assign ongoing maintenance

Decide who reviews updates, checks contact delivery, and monitors renewal notices. Separate tasks handled by the host from tasks still owned by your team.

For anyone researching “hosting wordpress,” that responsibility split matters as much as the hosting label. Choose a compatible environment, verify the recovery process, and pay for the service that fits the website’s real needs.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between web hosting and WordPress hosting?

Web hosting is the broader service that runs or publishes a website, while WordPress hosting is an offer intended for WordPress sites. The label alone does not establish which updates, backups, or maintenance tasks are included.

### Do I get hosting on WordPress.org?

WordPress.org provides the software and related resources rather than a hosting account for your self-hosted installation. WordPress.com is a separate hosted service, so decide which route you mean before purchasing additional hosting.

### Is managed WordPress hosting worth paying for?

It can be worthwhile when the included operational tools and support reduce work your team would otherwise need to handle. Compare the actual scope, resource limits, and renewal cost rather than assuming “managed” means every website task is covered.

### When should I change my WordPress host?

Consider a change when verified compatibility, resource, recovery, or support needs exceed the current arrangement. Identify the cause first so you do not migrate a software problem that the new host will not solve.
