# Best Email Marketing Software in 2026 (Compared)

The best email marketing software depends on your audience size, sending frequency, and the customer journey you need to support. Evaluate MailerLite for a small newsletter list, Brevo for its daily sending model, Mailchimp for a compact campaign setup, and Klaviyo when ecommerce data and automated messages are central to your requirements.

## Quick picks

This shortlist uses published product information and practical buying criteria, rather than claimed hands-on results. The suggested uses help you choose what to evaluate; they do not establish that one provider delivers better results for every business.

### For a small newsletter: MailerLite

MailerLite's [current Free plan](https://www.mailerlite.com/pricing) includes up to 250 subscribers and 2,500 monthly emails. That makes it worth shortlisting when your starting list fits the allowance and regular newsletters are the main requirement.

Check current information rather than relying on older reviews. MailerLite's [free-plan update](https://www.mailerlite.com/help/free-plan-update-faq) documents reductions in both subscriber and sending allowances.

### For modest daily sending: Brevo

Brevo's [Free plan](https://help.brevo.com/hc/en-us/articles/208589409-About-Brevo-s-pricing-plans) includes 300 email sends daily. It is a candidate when that daily schedule matches your needs, rather than when a large audience must receive one announcement at the same time.

Compare storage, sending, and automation allowances separately. A large contact-storage figure does not mean you can email the entire list in one campaign under the daily cap.

### For a compact starting campaign: Mailchimp

Mailchimp's [Free Marketing plan](https://mailchimp.com/help/about-mailchimp-pricing-plans/) supports 250 contacts and 500 monthly sends, with a daily limit of 250. It is worth evaluating when your initial audience and campaign schedule fit all three limits.

Do not choose it based only on contact count. Include welcome messages and other planned sends when estimating whether the allowance is sufficient.

### For store-connected automation: Klaviyo

Klaviyo's [flow documentation](https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/115002774932) describes automated communications and flow templates organised around marketing goals, ecommerce integrations, and channels. Shortlist it when store-related customer journeys are a central part of your evaluation.

Its [free email plan](https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/360050759151) allows up to 250 active profiles and 500 monthly email sends. Treat that as a starting allowance, then calculate the package required for your actual customer base.

## Reviews

### MailerLite: evaluate it for a small, regular newsletter

MailerLite's [pricing page](https://www.mailerlite.com/pricing) lists campaigns, automations, signup forms, and a landing page among its free capabilities. The published Free allowance also includes two user seats, alongside the subscriber and monthly sending limits above.

The practical attraction is a starting package you can assess against a defined newsletter workflow. Write down how subscribers join, what they receive first, and how often you plan to send updates.

**Suggested advantage:** its published monthly allowance is worth examining for a small list receiving repeated updates. A fictional list of 200 subscribers receiving four newsletters would use 800 sends, before counting automation or other messages.

That calculation is only a planning example. Add every planned message and check the account's actual usage before assuming the full workflow fits.

**Main caution:** old plan descriptions can be misleading. Use the current subscriber, sending, and feature limits rather than assuming an allowance from a previous review remains available.

For evaluation, prepare one newsletter using fictional business details. Preview the message, check the form-to-subscriber journey, and confirm what the selected permanent plan includes after any trial ends.

Ask a colleague to edit the same draft if shared work matters. Decide who approves it and whether the available access arrangement suits your team.

**Shortlist verdict:** consider MailerLite when your starting audience is small and you can describe a regular newsletter process within the current limits.

### Brevo: evaluate the daily allowance against your schedule

Brevo's [plan guide](https://help.brevo.com/hc/en-us/articles/208589409-About-Brevo-s-pricing-plans) lists an email editor, templates, basic reporting, and automation capabilities with stated limits. Free includes 300 daily email sends and contact storage for up to 100,000 contacts.

Those figures answer different questions. The storage allowance concerns information in the account, while the daily send allowance constrains how you communicate with that audience.

**Suggested advantage:** the plan is worth evaluating when your sending routine fits a modest daily volume. Prepare a calendar rather than judging it solely by how many contacts it can store.

**Main caution:** unused daily sends do not roll over. Brevo's [free-plan limits article](https://help.brevo.com/hc/en-us/articles/208580669-FAQs-What-are-the-limits-of-the-Free-plan) also explains restrictions after reaching the daily allowance.

For example, a fictional business wanting to send the same announcement to 600 people on one day would exceed the free daily cap. Dividing the audience changes the campaign schedule, so decide whether that is acceptable or whether a paid package is appropriate.

Use an evaluation to check the complete message journey, including signup, the first communication, and any planned follow-up. Confirm which automation and user allowances belong to your chosen plan.

Request a paid estimate based on realistic sending volume and contact requirements. Include any other communication channel you genuinely need as a separate requirement.

**Shortlist verdict:** consider Brevo when its sending model matches your routine and you have checked the limits beyond contact storage.

### Mailchimp: assess the exact starting workload

Mailchimp's [plan documentation](https://mailchimp.com/help/about-mailchimp-pricing-plans/) lists one audience, one owner seat, selected templates, and basic reporting in Free. It distinguishes that package from its paid marketing plans.

The right question is whether that specific setup supports your first campaigns. Familiar branding or a broad product catalogue should not replace checking the package you intend to use.

**Suggested advantage:** it is a relevant candidate for evaluating a small, straightforward campaign process. Prepare the content and audience before opening the editor so the evaluation has a clear purpose.

**Main caution:** the free monthly send total can become restrictive before the contact limit changes. A fictional list of 200 contacts receiving three full-list campaigns would require 600 sends, exceeding the stated 500 monthly allowance.

Ask how your contacts are counted for the proposed paid plan and obtain an estimate at your likely next list size. Check the required features separately from that contact tier.

For setup, follow Mailchimp's current [domain authentication instructions](https://mailchimp.com/help/set-up-email-domain-authentication/). Involve the person responsible for your domain rather than copying settings from an unrelated guide.

During evaluation, inspect the preview and links in a sample message. Mailchimp's [email best practices](https://mailchimp.com/help/best-practices-mailchimp-email/) describes preview and testing tools for checking those details.

**Shortlist verdict:** consider Mailchimp when the exact plan and audience rules suit your workflow, with enough sending capacity for the full schedule.

### Klaviyo: evaluate customer journeys alongside the store connection

Klaviyo documents flows as automated communications, including templates selected around ecommerce integrations and marketing goals. Its [segmentation guidance](https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/360035312491) also explains working with customer information to define audiences.

That makes it a relevant shortlist option for ecommerce email marketing. Evaluate it using your store's required data and customer journey rather than only a newsletter design exercise.

**Suggested advantage:** its documented flow model gives you a concrete structure to examine for messages tied to customer activity. Begin with one journey and establish what data makes a person eligible.

**Main caution:** profile counts and sending capacity require separate attention. Use the current [pricing information](https://www.klaviyo.com/pricing) and help documentation to confirm the package for your store.

For a fictional shop, you might evaluate a post-purchase educational message about using a product. Check what triggers the message, how the correct product information is selected, and when someone should stop receiving the sequence.

Confirm the integration's behaviour using test records before relying on live customer information. A connector being available does not establish that your particular data and workflow are configured correctly.

Also decide how you will review results against actual orders and customer interactions. A platform report should support a business question rather than become a collection of numbers nobody uses.

**Shortlist verdict:** consider Klaviyo when store-connected journeys justify the setup and ongoing maintenance required.

## How to choose

### Define the email job first

Write a one-sentence requirement: “Send a monthly update to customers who requested it” or “Provide product guidance after a purchase.” Different requirements call for different evaluation scenarios.

A small-business newsletter and a store's automated customer journeys are not identical buying problems. Choose two email platforms whose documented capabilities match the job you can define.

If you need an email marketing platform for small business, begin with your actual schedule and team. Resist expanding the requirements just because a demonstration includes more features.

### Calculate contacts and sends separately

A send generally means one message to one recipient; MailerLite explains this explicitly in its pricing FAQ. A campaign sent to 200 recipients therefore uses 200 sends, and four such campaigns use 800 before any other messages.

List newsletters, welcome emails, automated follow-ups, and other communications separately. Check how the selected provider counts each one and how its billing cycle resets.

Then record the daily limits as well as the monthly allowance. A plan can have enough monthly capacity while still failing your intended one-day announcement.

### Test one complete workflow

Use fictional details first and document the package being evaluated. Create the signup form, prepare the first message, and inspect the destination page and any follow-up.

For an online shop, add a suitable test customer event and review eligibility and stopping conditions. Confirm that the proposed sequence matches what the customer should reasonably expect.

Ask the future operator to complete the work with minimal assistance. Note confusing steps, missing capabilities, and anything that depends on another subscription.

### Check the tools you need to connect

List your website platform, store, CRM, and any other essential system. For each connection, ask what information moves, in which direction, and how updates or failures are handled.

Do not assume that a form submission should become both a sales enquiry and a marketing subscription. Define the intended journey and make the wording and records match that choice.

If enquiries need shared follow-up, review our [CRM comparison](https://futureforwardstudios.com/best-crm-for-small-businesses/) alongside the email requirements.

### Map an automation before enabling it

Draw the sequence in plain language before building it: what starts the journey, who qualifies, which message follows, and what ends it. Give each message a specific purpose rather than filling a template with repeated promotions.

For a fictional workshop business, the first message might explain how to prepare, while a later message answers a common question. Decide what should happen if the customer cancels, changes their booking, or no longer wants the messages.

Ask the provider to demonstrate the necessary conditions in the exact package you are considering. Treat an unresolved stopping rule as a setup task to finish before launch, rather than something to investigate after customers receive the sequence.

### Judge the evaluation honestly

Do not use a handful of preview messages to declare that one platform has the best inbox placement. This article makes no comparative deliverability claim, and its recommendations should not be read as one.

Instead, record what your evaluation actually establishes: whether the workflow is understandable, the required information transfers correctly, and the finished message matches the approved brief. Keep unanswered questions separate from confirmed capabilities.

After launch, review delivery issues and customer responses through the provider's reports and support process. Investigate the relevant evidence before attributing a problem or an improvement to the software alone.

### Review support and day-to-day ownership

Identify who writes, approves, sends, and reviews each campaign. Check whether the selected plan supports the required users and the help your team may need.

Maintain a short checklist for sender details, links, dates, conditions, and the recipient group. Review the whole message in context instead of approving only the subject line or design.

For AI-assisted drafting, keep approved facts separate from generated suggestions. Our [AI marketing guide](https://futureforwardstudios.com/how-to-use-ai-in-marketing/) provides a practical review process.

### Compare growth costs before migrating

Request quotes for your current list and a realistic next stage, using the same send volume and required functions. Include the billing term, extra users, essential add-ons, and any migration work.

Ask what happens when you exceed an allowance. Establish whether sending pauses, additional charges apply, or the account moves to another tier under the selected terms.

Before committing, check how you would export the information you need if you moved again. Keep records of your signup sources and subscription status as part of the migration plan.

### Make the final decision

Choose the email promotion service that completes your essential workflow at a sustainable cost. Shortlist MailerLite for a small regular newsletter, Brevo for a suitable daily schedule, Mailchimp for a compact campaign setup, and Klaviyo for store-connected journeys.

The best email marketing software should fit both the messages you send now and the next stage you can reasonably foresee. Start with a manageable process, confirm the plan limits, and upgrade when a defined requirement justifies it.

## FAQ

### What is the best email marketing software for a small business?

The right choice depends on list size, sending frequency, and required workflows rather than a universal winner. Compare MailerLite, Brevo, and Mailchimp for your campaign routine, and include Klaviyo when store-connected customer journeys are a priority.

### Is there free email marketing software?

All four providers in this guide publish free options with specific limits. MailerLite currently lists 250 subscribers and 2,500 monthly emails, Brevo lists 300 daily sends, and Mailchimp and Klaviyo each list 500 monthly sends with their respective contact or active-profile limits.

### Which email platform should an ecommerce business evaluate?

Klaviyo is worth shortlisting because its documentation explicitly covers flows and ecommerce-related customer journeys. Compare the required store connection, message eligibility, stopping conditions, and total cost with another suitable candidate before choosing.

### When should I upgrade from a free email plan?

Upgrade when a defined requirement exceeds the current sending, audience, user, or feature allowance. Confirm the full paid cost and what happens at future limits before moving your workflow.
