# SEO Report Generators and Free Templates for Small Sites

An SEO report generator can assemble useful charts and metrics, but a small site’s report also needs a clear explanation and a short action list. Start with your own search data and the template below, then pay for additional reporting features when they solve a recurring problem.

## What to include in a report

### Start with the website’s goal

Write one sentence explaining what the site is trying to achieve. A service business might want relevant enquiries, while a publication might focus on helping readers find and use its guides.

That goal determines which information belongs in the report. A chart should help someone understand progress or decide what to do next, rather than appear simply because the software offers it.

Name the intended reader too. A site owner, editor, and technical maintainer may need different detail even when they work on the same website.

### Define the period and scope

State the reporting dates, comparison dates, website property, and important filters. Keep the selected country, device, search type, and page group clear where those affect the figures.

Use a consistent reporting period so readers understand what changed. Note unusual events such as a migration, tracking change, or major promotion that may affect interpretation.

If the data is incomplete, say so. Missing tracking history cannot be repaired by a more attractive dashboard.

### Include search visibility and useful outcomes

Search Console’s performance report includes clicks, impressions, click-through rate, and average position. It also supports views such as queries, pages, countries, and devices. [Search Console performance documentation](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7576553?hl=en).

Choose the views relevant to the goal rather than displaying every table. A focused topic group may explain more than a site-wide total.

Keep business outcomes separate from search metrics. An enquiry, subscription, or purchase needs an appropriate measurement source and definition; search clicks alone do not establish those results.

### Explain the work completed

List meaningful content and technical changes during the period. Include the affected pages and the reason for the work.

Distinguish a published article from a completed improvement. If a broken contact journey was corrected, record how it was checked rather than only saying the website was updated.

For a technical work queue, use [our SEO audit checklist](https://futureforwardstudios.com/seo-audit-checklist/). The reporting summary should highlight the important consequences instead of copying an entire crawl export.

### End with actions and owners

Choose a small set of next steps. Give each one an owner, a reason, and a completion check.

A report is useful when it changes a decision or clarifies responsibility. A large collection of screenshots does not automatically achieve that.

### Search Console: begin with the source data

Google describes Search Console as a free service for monitoring and troubleshooting your site’s search presence. It is an owner-focused source of information rather than a general-purpose report on another publisher’s private data. [About Search Console](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/9128668?hl=en).

For a small website, you can begin by reviewing relevant reports and recording the important observations in a simple document. Add the period, context, and actions rather than sharing an unexplained chart.

**Our suggested use:** A straightforward reporting routine where the owner needs a clear view of their own search presence. Start here before assuming a paid dashboard is required.

**What to check:** Make sure the report uses the intended property and filters. Do not treat average position as a permanent location every searcher sees.

### Google Data Studio: build a reusable dashboard

Google’s current documentation calls the product Data Studio, formerly Looker Studio, and describes it as a no-cost tool for customizable dashboards and reports. Its Search Console connector supports reporting on the site’s Google Search performance. [Data Studio introduction](https://docs.cloud.google.com/data-studio/welcome), [Search Console connector](https://docs.cloud.google.com/data-studio/connect-to-search-console).

**Our suggested use:** A site owner who wants a recurring dashboard with consistent charts and filters. Begin with one reliable data source and add others only when they answer a useful question.

**What to check:** Understand sharing permissions and how the data source is connected. A dashboard that works for its creator may need different access settings for another viewer.

Also separate the no-cost reporting interface from the cost or conditions of other connected services. The dashboard does not redefine the underlying data or establish that every integration is free.

### Ahrefs Report Builder: present the platform’s data

Ahrefs describes Report Builder as a way to assemble reports with widgets and written commentary. Its page lists one monthly report and five widgets for Ahrefs Free or Starter, with different allowances on other plans. [Ahrefs Report Builder](https://ahrefs.com/report-builder).

The product page also advertises scheduled PDF delivery, but its plan table distinguishes which subscriptions include scheduling. Do not assume every advertised function belongs to the free account.

**Our suggested use:** A team already using relevant Ahrefs data that wants a consistent report format. Evaluate the actual information available to the selected account rather than adding a tool purely for presentation.

**What to check:** Confirm the report allowance, widgets, and delivery options. Keep provider estimates distinct from first-party performance data when both appear in a report.

### Semrush My Reports: check the report tier

Semrush’s official reporting information describes Base and Pro reports, including a pay-per-report model. Its materials list US$10 per month for Base and US$20 for Pro, with certain Base reports included when purchasing a paid toolkit. [Semrush reporting guide](https://www.semrush.com/blog/creating-reports-with-semrush-report-builder/), [My Reports documentation](https://www.semrush.com/kb/34-my-reports).

**Our suggested use:** A business already doing recurring analysis in the platform and needing supported information in one report. Check which report type includes the required sources and presentation features.

**What to check:** Include the underlying toolkit and required add-ons in the budget. A per-report charge does not establish the full price of accessing every data source you intend to use.

Choose the reporting arrangement after defining the output. A polished PDF is not a reason to purchase unrelated capabilities nobody will maintain.

## Free template

### Copy this monthly report structure

Use the following headings in a document, spreadsheet, or dashboard. Replace the instructions with your own confirmed information; they are template fields rather than claims about a real site.

**Website and reporting owner**

Enter the website, report author, and person responsible for the next decisions. State the business or editorial goal in one sentence.

**Reporting period and scope**

Enter the start and end dates, comparison period, sources, property, and important filters. Note any incomplete data or measurement changes.

**Summary**

Write three short points: what changed, why it matters, and what needs attention next. Separate confirmed observations from explanations still being investigated.

**Search performance**

| Relevant business outcome | Enter measured value if available | Enter comparable value | Name the measurement source |

**Page and topic review**

List the pages or topic groups most relevant to the goal. Explain the question each observation raises rather than pasting a long keyword export.

**Work completed**

Record important changes, affected URLs, reasons, and validation. Distinguish finished work from tasks that were only started.

**Issues and next actions**

| Action | Reason | Owner | Completion check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Enter a specific task | Explain the verified need | Assign one responsible person | State how completion will be confirmed |

**Notes and open questions**

Record unusual events, incomplete evidence, and decisions that need another person’s input. Carry unresolved actions into the next review.

### Add a focused SEO keyword report

Group queries by the audience task or topic they represent. Separate branded navigation from broader discovery where that distinction helps interpret the data.

Connect important query groups to the relevant pages. Ask whether those pages provide the expected answer rather than treating every phrase as a new publishing assignment.

Use [our keyword research comparison](https://futureforwardstudios.com/best-keyword-research-tools/) to distinguish discovery tools from monitoring your own website. The report should preserve that difference.

### A worked example with fictional figures

Imagine a service site records 120 search clicks this month and 100 in the comparison period. The increase is 20 clicks, or 20 percent, calculated as the difference divided by the comparison value.

These are illustrative numbers, not results from Future Forward Studios or a client. They show why both the percentage and the underlying amount belong in an explanation.

A cautious summary might say: “Search clicks increased in the selected period. Review the affected pages and query groups before deciding what contributed to the change.”

That observation is more defensible than claiming a recent title edit caused the entire increase. A reporting tool can show a movement without establishing its cause.

### Explain rates without hiding the underlying counts

A rate needs context as well as a percentage. In a fictional example, 10 clicks from 200 impressions produces a five-percent click-through rate; 20 clicks from 1,000 impressions produces two percent.

The second period has more clicks but a lower rate. That combination is not automatically good or bad: investigate which queries and pages contributed before deciding what action follows.

Avoid averaging percentages from separate rows without understanding their underlying counts. If your data source provides the appropriate aggregate, use it consistently and preserve its definition in the report.

Also distinguish a percentage-point change from a relative percentage change. Moving from two percent to three percent is one percentage point higher, while the relative increase is fifty percent.

These illustrative calculations help prevent a dramatic-looking chart from misleading the reader. They do not establish any actual website’s performance.

### Check the report before sharing

Open the finished version as the intended reader would see it. Confirm that headings explain the scope and that important charts have understandable labels.

Check dates, selected filters, source connections, and whether any table contains stale or missing information. A copied template can retain an old date even when some charts have refreshed.

Review sharing access deliberately. A report should reach the intended people without exposing information more broadly than necessary.

Then read the written summary against the data. Remove claims of causation that the evidence does not support and replace vague recommendations with specific next actions.

Finally, confirm that every assigned task has an owner and a clear completion check. The reader should know what happens next without needing a separate conversation to decode the report.

Keep a dated copy of the version that was shared. That gives future reviews a stable reference instead of relying on a dashboard whose contents may change later.

### Keep the template small enough to use

Do not fill every available space with charts. Each section should support a decision or explain a meaningful observation.

If nobody uses a section, simplify it or remove it. A maintainable SEO analysis template should make the reporting process clearer, not create another publishing project every month.

## How often to report

### Choose a rhythm around decisions

A monthly review is a practical starting suggestion for a small site, not a universal rule. Adjust the rhythm to the publishing schedule, available data, and decisions the team needs to make.

Use shorter checks for operational problems such as a broken enquiry route. Do not wait for the next formal report to investigate an issue affecting customers now.

For a substantial migration or change, define the specific monitoring needed. Keep that separate from the routine summary so an unusual event does not confuse every comparison.

### Keep comparisons consistent

Use the same source definitions and filters where possible. Explain differences in period length, incomplete information, or other conditions that affect interpretation.

Be cautious with percentages when the underlying numbers are small. Always include enough context for the reader to understand the scale of a movement.

If the comparison period is zero, a normal percentage-change calculation is not defined. Describe the absolute change rather than displaying an invented or misleading percentage.

### Review the actions, not just the charts

Begin each reporting session by checking the previous action list. Confirm what was completed, what remains open, and what evidence changed the plan.

Retain the completion checks. A reported issue should not disappear simply because a newer report contains a different warning.

Use [our audit checklist](https://futureforwardstudios.com/seo-audit-checklist/) for technical and content review, and [competitor keyword research](https://futureforwardstudios.com/competitor-keyword-research/) when the next task concerns audience opportunities.

### Automate carefully

Automate collection or delivery only after the report’s sources and interpretation are understood. Check permissions, recipients, selected dates, and whether the report refreshes as intended.

Keep a person responsible for reviewing the summary and recommendations. Repeated delivery of an unclear dashboard does not make the information more useful.

The right SEO report generator saves repetitive work while leaving a clear explanation of what happened and what comes next. Start with the template, reliable source data, and a short action list, then add software when the recurring benefit is clear.

## FAQ

### Can I create an SEO report for free?

Yes: you can use your own Search Console data and a simple document, or investigate Google Data Studio’s no-cost reporting option. Account access, connected services, and the information you need still determine the complete setup.

### What should a small website’s SEO report include?

Include the goal, reporting scope, relevant performance observations, completed work, and prioritized next actions. Keep sources and limitations clear so readers understand what the numbers establish.

### Do I need a paid SEO report generator?

Not necessarily: begin with the recurring task and the free workflow you can maintain. Pay when required integrations, report volume, presentation, or delivery capabilities justify the total cost.

### How often should I send an SEO report?

Choose a schedule that supports real decisions, with monthly reviews as one practical starting point. Investigate urgent operational issues separately rather than waiting for a scheduled summary.
