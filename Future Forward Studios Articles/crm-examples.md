# CRM Examples: Real Uses and Software Examples

Examples of CRM include recording a website enquiry, scheduling a quote follow-up, and sharing customer information when work passes between colleagues. HubSpot, Zoho CRM, Pipedrive, and Salesforce are software examples; the practical value comes from using a suitable system to manage a clear customer process.

## Everyday CRM use cases: examples of CRM in practice

CRM stands for customer relationship management. If the concept is new to you, our [plain-English CRM guide](https://futureforwardstudios.com/what-does-crm-mean/) explains how customer records, opportunities, and tasks fit together.

The workflows below are illustrative examples, not reports of our own product testing. They show what to evaluate in a system rather than promising that every product includes every function.

### Recording and assigning an enquiry

Imagine a fictional cleaning company receiving a request for an office quote. A useful working record would include the contact, the requested service, the employee responsible, and the next action.

The key question is whether someone owns the response. Storing the person's email address is only the beginning of that process.

If the enquiry comes through a website form, check how it reaches the CRM. Confirm the required connection in your selected plan rather than assuming that the transfer happens automatically.

### Following up after a proposal

A fictional web design agency sends a proposal and agrees to call the prospect the following week. The working process should record that commitment with a named owner and a date.

When the owner reviews open opportunities, the next action should be clear. “Discuss proposal on Tuesday” is more useful than an undated note saying “interested.”

Also define when to close an opportunity. If the prospect declines, record the outcome instead of leaving an old proposal among active sales discussions.

### Passing information to another colleague

Imagine a salesperson handing a new customer to the person delivering the service. The handoff should include the agreed scope, main contact, important commitments, and any unresolved questions.

Ask the second person to explain the customer's request using the available record. If they need to reconstruct it from private messages, improve the handoff before adding more software.

### A documented example: agency follow-ups

Pipedrive's [SoMe Connect case study](https://www.pipedrive.com/en/case-studies/some-connect-case-study) describes the agency tracking leads, recording follow-ups, and evaluating outcomes. This is a vendor-published customer account, rather than an independently measured result from Future Forward Studios.

The useful lesson is the workflow: make conversations and next actions visible. It does not establish that another agency will achieve the same outcome simply by purchasing the product.

## Software examples

These examples of CRM software illustrate different tools you can investigate. This section explains capabilities, rather than ranking plans or comparing prices.

### HubSpot: contacts, deals, and tasks

HubSpot lists contact, deal, and task management on its [CRM product page](https://www.hubspot.com/products/crm). Those capabilities provide a starting point for evaluating how to connect a person, a possible sale, and the next action.

For an example of CRM evaluation, create a fictional contact and potential project, then assign a follow-up task. Ask a colleague to find the record and explain who should act next.

Check the current plan limits before inviting the team or importing records. A capability listed somewhere in a provider's product range is not evidence that it belongs to every subscription.

### Zoho CRM: leads, follow-ups, and reports

Zoho's [Free Edition overview](https://www.zoho.com/crm/zohocrm-pricing-free.html) describes lead management, workflow examples, and built-in reporting. It is a useful product to examine when your evaluation includes both customer follow-ups and reviewing sales activity.

Use a sample enquiry to check how the selected edition represents your process. Then ask which report would answer a specific question, such as which open opportunities need attention.

Do not build reports before agreeing on the meaning of your sales stages. Consistent records are part of the setup work, whichever product you choose.

### Pipedrive: moving from a lead to a deal

Pipedrive's [lead and deal documentation](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/leads-vs-deals) explains converting a qualified lead into a deal in a sales pipeline. It also describes transferring linked information into the new deal.

That makes it a concrete software example for a business distinguishing an early enquiry from an active sales opportunity. Define your own qualification rule before deciding where that boundary should sit.

For example, you might require a confirmed need and an agreed next conversation. Treat this as a proposed business rule, rather than a universal CRM requirement.

### Salesforce Starter Suite: connected customer activities

Salesforce describes [Starter Suite](https://www.salesforce.com/small-business/starter/) as bringing sales, service, marketing, commerce, and Slack together. It is a relevant example when you want to evaluate work spanning more than one customer-facing role.

Ask for a demonstration of your specific handoff, using the package you intend to buy. Confirm which information carries through and which actions require another product or additional setup.

## Examples by industry

The scenarios below are fictional and intended to help you design a process. They are not customer case studies or claims about a particular product's industry suitability.

### Home services: enquiry to scheduled work

A fictional plumbing business could track a request from first contact through a visit, quote, and customer decision. Each stage should show who is responsible and what information is still needed.

Useful details might include the service address, the reported issue, and the agreed callback time. Decide separately where job scheduling, invoicing, and completed work records belong.

Use the CRM evaluation to check the customer handoff. Do not assume a general CRM replaces specialist tools your team already depends on.

### Professional services: proposal to project handoff

A fictional marketing consultancy might need to track discovery calls, proposals, and decisions. Once work is agreed, the delivery team needs the approved scope and any promises made during the sale.

Keep possible work separate from confirmed work in your process. That distinction helps you review open opportunities without treating every conversation as a booked project.

Record the next customer-facing action throughout the handoff. Avoid marking a deal complete while leaving the customer unsure who will contact them.

### Retail or wholesale: repeat orders

A fictional wholesale supplier could organise conversations around company accounts and their purchasing contacts. Its process might include recording the last discussion, an upcoming purchasing review, and unresolved service questions.

Before choosing software, decide whether order information must connect to an existing commerce or accounting system. Confirm that connection rather than assuming a CRM duplicates all those functions.

### Membership businesses: enquiry to joining

A fictional fitness studio might track an enquiry, a visit, and the person's decision about joining. Assign responsibility for the next conversation so the prospect does not receive conflicting messages.

A related real-world example appears in Pipedrive's [Evanston Athletic Club case study](https://www.pipedrive.com/en/case-studies/evanston-case-study), which describes tracking interactions from walk-ins and web enquiries. It illustrates the use case without proving that the same configuration suits every membership business.

## Next steps

### Choose one workflow to improve

Start with a specific problem, such as proposals lacking follow-up dates. Describe the intended result in one sentence before looking at advanced functions.

Write down the minimum information someone needs to complete that workflow. Include an owner, a status, and a next action where relevant.

### Evaluate with fictional records

Run the same scenario in two shortlisted tools and record the exact plan used. Ask the future users to complete the process themselves and note anything unclear.

Before importing live customer information, review access, export options, and your proposed field mapping. Begin with a small sample rather than moving everything at once.

### Review the process after launch

Choose one person to maintain field definitions and answer basic questions. During regular team reviews, look for missing owners, unclear stages, and tasks that no longer reflect the customer conversation.

Simplify fields that nobody uses and clarify responsibilities before adding more features. Keep a short record of changes so colleagues understand how to maintain the information.

The most useful examples of CRM connect customer information with a clear responsibility. Choose a tool that supports your process, then keep the records accurate enough for the next person to act.

## FAQ

### What is a simple example of CRM?

Recording a customer enquiry, assigning someone to respond, and scheduling the next conversation is a simple example. The process connects the customer's information with a clear action and owner.

### What are examples of CRM software?

HubSpot, Zoho CRM, Pipedrive, and Salesforce are examples of CRM software. Compare the exact product and plan against your workflow, since capabilities and limits differ.

### Can a small business use CRM without automation?

You can start by manually recording contacts, opportunities, and follow-up tasks in a suitable system. Establish a consistent process before deciding which repeated actions are worth automating.

### Is a customer contact list the same as CRM?

A contact list can be part of managing customer relationships, but it does not by itself describe ownership, commitments, or next actions. Use those missing details to decide whether your current process needs improvement.
